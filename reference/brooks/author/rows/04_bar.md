# Slice 04 (bar anatomy: trend / doji) — CATALOG rows

| `AverageTailFractionBelow` | Entry | same | `tailBars - 1` | `tailBars(5)`, `maxTailFraction(0.25)`, `epsilon(0.000001)` | The mean NON-BODY fraction of the last N bars, current bar included, is at or below T: `Average((High - Low - AbsValue(Close - Open)) / (High - Low + ε), tailBars)`. Both tails together and no direction at all, so both sides are identical text — Brooks's "bars with no tails or small tails in EITHER direction", the urgency sign. Not `AverageUpperTailFraction`, which averages ONE tail and mirrors the sides: a bar with a huge lower tail and a shaved top passes that rule's long side and fails this one; checked against its JSON and kept. Not `TailBarCountDominance` (a CountIF of tail-vs-body bars contesting the two directions) and not `AvgBodyBelowRangeFraction` (a ratio of two window means, where this averages the per-bar ratio). A plain `Average` over a price EXPRESSION, so a fresh loop with real history — not `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` and not a zero-filled variable mirror. The ε is `CloseRangePosition`'s literal carried as an input, guarding EL's runtime divide-by-zero on a zero-range bar |
| `BarRangeHighestOfWindow` | Entry | same | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(5)` | This bar's high−low range is at or above the largest range of the last N bars, the window INCLUDING the current bar — so it is a rank test that is true on any bar tying the window maximum. `Highest(High - Low, lookback)`, written out as the `Extremes` loop it bottoms out in: seeded at bar 0, scanning 1..Length−1, strict `el_gt` because Extremes is EL source. The `=` trap applies as in `Breakout` — the C++ is `>=`, never `==`. Not `BarRangeAboveStd` (mean + K sd over a mirrored variable history, which disagrees whenever the window already holds a big bar) and not `LargestTrendBarOfTheRun`, whose window ends ONE BAR AGO and which also demands a strong trend body. The `Highest` argument is a price expression with real history, so no zero-filled mirror |
| `BodyAboveAverageMultiple` | Entry | mirrored | `consecutiveBars + bodyLookback - 1` | `bodyLookback(10)`, `bodyMultiple(1.0)`, `consecutiveBars(1)` | Each of the last `consecutiveBars` bars is a bull bar (short: bear) whose body is at least K × the mean ABSOLUTE body of the `bodyLookback` bars ENDING ONE BAR BEFORE IT. At `consecutiveBars = 1` it is Brooks's "body about the same size as or larger than the median body of the past five or 10 bars" — the MEAN replaces the median deliberately (a running sum instead of a sorted window; comparative size is the claim). SUPERSEDES slice 07's `BodyAboveAverageBody`, which is this rule at `consecutiveBars = 2..N`: the run length is an input rather than a second rule. The benchmark EXCLUDES the bar it judges, the convention `LargeOutsideBar` / `BarRangeBelowAverageMultiple` / `LargeTailedBarCount` set, and it shifts per counted bar — so the mean is an explicit summation, not `Average(...)[kk + 1]`, since a function-history reference at a loop-variable offset is unregistered. All-of-N written `StrongTrendBarRun`'s way (start true, let any failing bar clear the flag) so the loop runs unconditionally. Nothing in the catalog measures a body against other bars' BODIES: `BodyBelowAtrFraction` measures it against the ATR and `CloseRangePosition` against this bar's own range |
| `BullBearBodyDominanceInWindow` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(20)`, `dominanceMultiple(1.5)` | Over the last N bars, current bar included, the total size of the up bodies exceeds K × the total size of the down bodies (short: mirrored) — buying pressure by body size, not bar count. The body-weighted twin of `TrendBarCountDominanceInWindow` (slice 05): one big bull bar can carry the window here and cannot there, and both are kept until someone measures how often they disagree. No body-fraction test on the individual bars — the sum itself does the weighting, which is what makes it a different rule from the count form. A tie bar adds nothing to either side. One pass over price expressions with real history, so a fresh loop, newest-first; the sums live in the hook because EasyLanguage does not short-circuit |
| `DojiBar` | Entry | same | 0 | `dojiFraction(0.2)` | The bar's body is at most F of its OWN high−low range: Brooks's doji, his one-bar trading range. Fifteen restatements merged; book values 0.1–0.3, default 0.2. Direction-agnostic, so both sides are identical text, and the "body dominates its range" / "never use a doji as the signal bar" sources are this rule NEGATED, a per-placement toggle. Bar 0 IS the signal bar under the corpus convention, so the sources testing `Close[1]` need no lag input. Not `BodyBelowAtrFraction`, which measures the same body against the N-bar ATR — a small body on a small bar is a doji here and not there, so the two fire on different bars. Not `TrendBarBody` negated, which is direction-carrying and admits the opposite trend bar. A product, not a quotient: EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero and a zero-range bar is legal data (and is a doji) |
| `DojiCountInWindow` | Entry | same | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(10)`, `minCount(3)`, `dojiFraction(0.2)` | At least K of the last N bars, current bar included, are doji bars by `DojiBar`'s test — two-sided trading. `CountIF(AbsValue(Close - Open) <= dojiFraction * (High - Low), lookback)`, counted bars 0..N−1 with the condition as a true series. Direction-agnostic; "few dojis", Brooks's strong-trend sign, is this rule negated, which is why the two source senses are one rule. Also the cheapest available proxy for a tight-trading-range test. Same CountIF shape as `TailBarCountDominance` but with no opposing count and no direction |
| `LargeBarCountInWindow` | Entry | same | `max(lookback - 1, atrLookback + 1)` | `lookback(10)`, `minCount(2)`, `largeMultiple(1.5)`, `atrLookback(14)` | At least K of the last N bars had a high−low range of at least M × the CURRENT N-bar ATR — outsized bars are present, whatever direction they closed. Brooks states it negated ("not many large bars appear, not even large trend bars", the orderly-trend sign) and the negate toggle covers that. ONE ATR value for the whole window, read at the current bar: the count is an explicit loop rather than `CountIF` precisely so the WFSafe call is not re-evaluated at each counted offset, and so the stateful accumulator stays out of a condition (EL does not short-circuit). `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` rolling accumulator, two separate statements, re-seeded on a length change, same block as `BarRangeAtrMultiple`. Not `LargeRangeBarInWindow` (at-least-one against a per-bar EXCLUSIVE mean range, no gaps), not `LargeTrendBarCountInWindow` (adds a with-trend close), not `BarRangeAboveStd` (current bar only) |
| `StrongBodyBarCountInWindow` | Entry | mirrored | `windowBars - 1` | `windowBars(6)`, `minCount(3)`, `strongBodyFraction(0.6)` | At least K of the last N bars, current bar included, closed with a body of at least F of that bar's own range in the trade's direction — cumulative buying (short: selling) pressure without needing one dramatic bar. SUPERSEDES slice 11's `StrongTrendBarCount`, the K-of-N count form the `StrongTrendBarRun` row names. Not `StrongTrendBarRun`, which is ALL-of-N consecutive; not `OppositeBodyCountInWindow`, which counts bare up/down closes against a CAP with no body-size term; not `TailBarCountDominance`, which counts TAILS and contests the two directions. `CountIF` over bars 0..N−1 with the counted condition a true series on prices, so no zero-filled mirror. A tie bar counts for neither side. Products, not quotients |
| `TwoBarReversal` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `sizeRatio(0.5)` | A bear trend bar immediately followed by a bull bar that closes above the bear bar's RANGE midpoint `(High[1] + Low[1]) / 2` and whose own range is at least K × the bear bar's range — the pair read as one bull reversal bar (short: mirrored). Five book restatements merged on the midpoint form plus a single range-ratio input; book sizes 0.5 and 0.7, default 0.5. The size term is a plain range ratio, so a doji second bar of the right size still qualifies — the body test belongs to a separate `TrendBarBody` / `StrongTrendBar` placement. The body-midpoint reading (T11-05) and the overlap-fraction reading (T06-05) were not carried. Two-bar OHLC arithmetic, no state; the only divisor is the constant 2, so no ε guard is needed |
| `TwoBarReversalBreakoutEntry` | Entry | mirrored | 2 | `bodyFraction(0.5)` | A bear bar two bars ago, then a bull bar one bar ago whose body is at least F of its range, and THIS bar closes above the HIGHER of those two bars' highs (short: below the lower of the two lows). ## Not written

- `BarRangeAboveAtrMultiple` — duplicate of `BarRangeAtrMultiple` (slice 08, already on disk). Behaviourally identical: `High - Low >= rangeMultiple * WFSafe_AvgTrueRange(atrLookback)`, identical both sides, same two inputs under the same names; only the defaults differ (5 / 20 there, this slice's merge wanted 1.5 / 14). Slice 04's whole family — "large / climactic bar", "not climactic" negated, "small bar" negated — is reachable from the existing rule by moving `rangeMultiple` and toggling negate, so the merge's own argument applies to it. Suggest the orchestrator widen `BarRangeAtrMultiple`'s optimizer grid down to ~0.5 rather than add a second rule.
- `ConsecutiveTrendBars` — duplicate of `StrongTrendBarRun` (slice 08, already on disk). Identical condition (every one of the last N bars a with-trend bar whose body is at least `bodyFraction` of its own range), identical input names `consecutiveBars` / `bodyFraction`, identical defaults 3 / 0.6, identical all-of-N flag construction. Slice 11's `ConsecutiveTrendBarRun` was held back as a duplicate of this key; it is a duplicate of `StrongTrendBarRun` too, so the whole three-way group collapses onto the rule already on disk. The eight book run lengths (2, 3, 4, 5, 8) are all reachable on its `consecutiveBars` grid, and the "do not fade after N consecutive trend bars" sources are it negated.

## Notes for the orchestrator

- `AverageUpperTailFraction` (slice 02) was checked against `AverageTailFractionBelow` per the brief. NOT a duplicate: slice 02's rule averages ONE tail and mirrors the sides (long reads the upper tail, short the lower), while this one averages `High - Low - AbsValue(Close - Open)` — both tails together — and is direction-agnostic with identical sides. They disagree on any bar with one large tail and one shaved end. Both kept.
- `BodyAboveAverageMultiple` absorbs slice 07's `BodyAboveAverageBody` via the `consecutiveBars` input (default 1); `StrongBodyBarCountInWindow` supersedes slice 11's `StrongTrendBarCount`. Neither superseded key should be authored.
- `LargeBarCountInWindow` is the second WFSafe call site added by this slice alongside `BarRangeAtrMultiple` / `ClimaxBar` / `AtrProfitTarget`; nothing new is assumed about it.
