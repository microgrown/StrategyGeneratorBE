| `TrailStopBehindStrongBar` | Exit | mirrored | 0 | `strongBodyFraction(0.6)`, `stopOffsetTicks(1)` | Each time a strong with-trend bar closes — body ≥ `strongBodyFraction` × its own high−low range — the protective level ratchets to that bar's NEAR extreme minus an offset (long: `Low − stopOffsetTicks × tick`), and the close at or below it takes the trade off; short is the mirror above the high. The level only ever moves TOWARD the trade and does not move at all through a run of weak bars, which is what separates it from `TrailingStop`/`AtrTrailingStop`, both of which measure a dollar or ATR distance below the position's PEAK PROFIT and so tighten on every new extreme. Not `TightenStopToEntryBarIfStrong`, which tests the ENTRY bar once and never moves again. Per position: the level and its have-flag are cleared on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED as 0 on the fill bar and as restarting through a reversal), never on a `MarketPosition = 0` test; the fill bar itself can seed the level, and a bar can never take itself out because the seed is its own low minus an offset. The ratchet is an `if`, not `MaxList`, since a seeded-or-not level needs the flag test anyway. R01-02's alternative "strong = range ≥ 1.2 × ATR" reading is dropped in favour of the body test, which needs no ATR call site; the body comparison is a multiplication, never a division, so a zero-range bar cannot raise EL's divide-by-zero |
| `TrailArmedAfterProfitThreshold` | Exit | mirrored | 1 | `armProfitDollars(100)`, `stopOffsetTicks(1)` | The trade has shown at least `armProfitDollars` of peak open profit and the close is now beyond the PRIOR bar's extreme (long: `Close ≤ Low[1] − stopOffsetTicks × tick`); before that the rule never fires and the wider initial stop, a separate placement, stands alone. R29-14 states it as a GATE on other tightening rules, which the pane system cannot express because exits are ORed and one exit cannot condition another — the armed-trail recast is the only mechanizable shape, the same recast `NumBarsIfProfitable` makes for the profit-gated time stop. Arming is `MaxPositionProfit`, not a hand-rolled flag: VERIFIED as the INTRABAR peak, net of the entry side's costs, starting at 0 per position and restarting through a reversal, so it is already a once-true-per-position latch and nothing needs resetting. `armProfitDollars` is CURRENCY — the source's "about four ticks" is a per-symbol statement of the same idea and the dollar form is the corpus's threshold currency — while the stop offset is genuinely in ticks. `TrailStopBeyondPriorBarExtreme` (slice T03) is this rule with the arming removed. The slice said sides `same`; the prior-bar extreme is directional, so they are mirrored, the same correction `BreakevenStop` and `BreakevenExitAfterAdverseExcursion` needed |
| `TrailArmedAfterEmaCloseBreak` | Exit | mirrored | 1 (the EMA is recursive state, not a window — its seed bar is part of the specification) | `emaLength(20)`, `stopOffsetTicks(1)` | The trail is armed structurally instead of on a dollar amount: once the close has printed on the wrong side of the EMA at least once SINCE ENTRY, a close beyond the prior bar's extreme (long: `Close ≤ Low[1] − stopOffsetTicks × tick`) takes the trade off; until then it never fires. R12-12's "do not use a tight stop while there have been no closes below the moving average" is a gate, and the same armed-trail recast applies. Brooks scopes the scan to "since the current leg began"; anchored to SINCE ENTRY, which is mechanizable and avoids the leg-definition dependency, and the source's `minBars = 10` is dropped as an artifact of the chart example. Not `BreakevenStopAfterEmaHoldingRun`, which counts N consecutive closes HOLDING on the right side of the average and arms a BREAKEVEN stop — that arms while the trade is going well, this on the first close going wrong, and the two arm on disjoint bars. Flags reset on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED), never on `MarketPosition = 0`; the fill bar can set them. The EMA is the `WFSafe_Xaverage` mirror carried by `EmaGapBar` — price seed on the first calculated bar, `x + sf*(p − x)` as ONE statement, `sf = 2/(emaLength+1)`, no warm-up hole; `emaLength` may be optimized in grids (the wrapper recomputes SF on a length change, measured 2026-09-17). Only the current value is read, so no lagged read and no mirrored ring buffer |
| `SwingPointTrailingStop` | Exit | mirrored | `lookbackBars − 1 + pivotStrength` | `pivotStrength(2)`, `trailOffsetTicks(1)`, `lookbackBars(40)` | The headline Brooks trail: a long is closed when the close falls to or below the most recent CONFIRMED swing low minus `trailOffsetTicks`, short mirrored above the last swing high. Nine mined candidates from all three volumes collapse here. Not `TrailingStop`/`AtrTrailingStop`, which trail a distance below PEAK PROFIT — on a trend that pauses the dollar trail tightens with every new high while this does not move until a new pivot confirms. Not `StopBeyondRecentRangeExtreme`, whose level is an N-bar lowest low through the previous bar, a Donchian floor that moves every bar the window rolls. Not `SwingPivotTarget`, a profit target at the OPPOSING pivot (a long exits at the swing HIGH); this is a stop at the SAME-side pivot. Not `InitialStopBeyondSwingAtEntry`, which freezes the pivot that existed at entry. Four sources add "only after a new high", dropped as redundant: a fresh swing low only becomes the most recent one after a rally, so the trigger adds no bars and would cost a `Highest` call site. THE LEVEL IS NOT RATCHETED — it is re-read every bar, so a deeper pullback printing a LOWER swing low widens the stop; Brooks says "raise the stop", but the merged pseudocode is the plain read and a per-position monotone ratchet is the machinery `TrailStopBehindStrongBar` and `StopBeyondExtremeSinceEntry` already carry. Pivot scan inlined from `SwingHigh` per the B-list decision: at or above the `pivotStrength` older bars, strictly above the `pivotStrength` newer ones, lag exactly `pivotStrength`, occurrence 1 only, `-1` sentinel guarded on the price, `el_gt`/`el_ge`. `lookbackBars` is not in the merged parameter list and is what makes the pivot window addressable (40 as in `SwingPivotTarget`) |
| `InitialStopBeyondSwingAtEntry` | Exit | mirrored | `stopLookback − 1 + pivotStrength` | `pivotStrength(2)`, `stopTicks(1)`, `stopLookback(10)` | Risk to the swing that existed when the trade was taken: the most recent confirmed swing low is captured ON THE FILL BAR and the level never moves, so a long exits when the close reaches `swingAtEntry − stopTicks × tick`. Brooks's stop for limit-order channel entries. Kept apart from `SwingPointTrailingStop`, which re-reads the pivot every bar — on a trend that runs the trailed rule exits far higher, and on a trend that never prints a new pivot the two are identical — and from `StopBeyondExtremeSinceEntry`, whose level is a running extreme that WIDENS as the trade goes against it. The PIVOT form is used rather than T15-47's `Lowest(Low, stopLookback)` proxy, which the merge notes suggested trying first: the swing primitive is MEASURED now, so the proxy buys nothing, and `stopLookback` therefore serves as the PIVOT WINDOW (EL's `Length`) rather than a lowest-low length — at the source's 10 with strength 2 the reportable offsets are 2..9. Captured on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED: 0 on the fill bar, restarting through a reversal), the same at-entry convention `StopAtSignalBarRangeMultiple` and `PullbackSizedProfitTarget` use, which is what holds "reaches back" at the pivot window rather than at the length of the longest trade. The `-1` sentinel is guarded by the have-flags — a captured `-1` as a price sits under every real price — and both scans run unconditionally every bar so Max Bars Back covers them |
| `DelaySwingTrailUntilRetestAndNewExtreme` | Exit | mirrored | `max(lookbackBars − 1 + pivotStrength, atrLookback + 1)` | `signalBarAreaAtr(0.25)`, `atrLookback(14)`, `pivotStrength(2)`, `trailOffsetTicks(1)`, `lookbackBars(40)` | The swing trail arms only after the trade has BOTH pulled back into the area of the signal bar (`Low ≤ sigHigh + signalBarAreaAtr × ATR`) and afterwards made a new extreme beyond its running high since entry; until that sequence completes the rule never fires and the wider initial stop stands, so the normal pullbacks of a tight channel cannot trap the trade out. Once armed it is `SwingPointTrailingStop`'s exit. The armed-trail recast again — T23-11 states a gate, and exits are ORed. The signal bar is captured on the fill bar as `High[1]`/`Low[1]`, the `StopAtSignalBarRangeMultiple` convention, NOT re-indexed by the position's age as the merged pseudocode's `High[BarsSinceEntry + 1]` would, which would make "reaches back" the length of the longest trade. THE FILL BAR CANNOT SUPPLY THE RETEST: the fill is at the open immediately after the signal bar, so that bar's low is inside the signal bar's area by construction and would hand the arming away for nothing — the retest test is gated on `BarsSinceEntry ≥ 1`. The running extreme is seeded on the fill bar and updated AFTER the tests, so a bar cannot beat itself. "Area of the signal bar" is an ATR fraction because Brooks gives no number; T23-11's own 0.25 is kept. ATR is MultiWalk's `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` rolling accumulator, two separate statements, re-seeded on a length change. Same arming idea as `BreakevenStopOnSecondEntryTest`, which arms a BREAKEVEN stop off a touch-recover-touch sequence at the ENTRY price: different reference, different bars, different level |
| `StopBeyondSpikeStart` | Exit | mirrored | 0 | `stopBufferTicks(1)`, `strongBodyFraction(0.6)`, `maxTailFraction(0.2)`, `consecutiveBars(2)` | For a trade entered during a spike the stop sits beyond the START of the spike — the first bar of the current run of consecutive strong trend bars — rather than beyond the signal or entry bar, so a long exits at `spikeLow − stopBufferTicks × tick`. Deliberately wider than `StopBeyondSignalBar`: the reference bar can be several bars before the entry. The spike comes from the SETTLED ALWAYS-IN DECISION, inlined rather than referenced: body ≥ `strongBodyFraction` × the bar's own range with BOTH tails ≤ `maxTailFraction` × it, `consecutiveBars` (default 2) in a row being the flip; the decision's 0.6/0.2/2 are promoted to inputs. The run's first bar's low is carried in a local while the run extends, so no bar index grows with the spike's length and the deepest bar touched is 0. THE LEVEL IS FROZEN AT ENTRY: the spike level is market state that keeps updating, but the stop is the spike the trade was ENTERED in, so it is captured on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED) and never moved, which makes this an initial stop rather than another trail; a trade entered before any spike has printed never fires this exit, which the have-flags carry. Body and tail tests are multiplications, never divisions, so a zero-range bar cannot raise EL's divide-by-zero. `depends_on_keys: AlwaysInDirection` (no rule file exists for it; the state machine is inlined per the standing decision) |
| `TrailStopToMicroMeasuringGap` | Exit | mirrored | 2 | `toleranceTicks(1)` | A bar whose low sits above the high of the bar TWO before it leaves a micro measuring gap, and the stop trails into it: the level is held, and a long exits when the close reaches `gapLevel − toleranceTicks × tick`; short is the mirror on a bar whose high is below the low of two bars ago. The gap test is trivial and all the machinery is in HOLDING the last gap's level, which is why the rule is carried whole rather than split into a gap filter. Not `MicroMeasuringGap`, the Entry, which also requires the middle bar to be a trend bar of a given body fraction and uses the INCLUSIVE boundary `Low >= High[2]` — this follows its own source's STRICT `Low > High[2]`, so the two differ on an exact touch — and not `LimitEntryAtMicroGapEdge`, a limit entry at the gap edge one bar later. The level is MARKET structure, not position state, so it survives across trades; a stale level is disarmed at entry unless it sits at or below the fill bar's close (mirrored for shorts), which is what stops an old bull gap left far above from taking a new long off on its own fill bar, while a gap printing during the trade arms unconditionally since such a level is at or below its own bar's close by construction. Armed on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED), and the arming is a bool rather than the merged pseudocode's `gapLevel > 0`, because a back-adjusted continuous contract can price below zero |
| `BreakevenStopOnSecondEntryTest` | Exit | mirrored | 0 | `awayTicks(4)` | Let the first pullback to the entry price go and exit at breakeven on the second: after price has touched the entry price, moved away in the trade's favour by `awayTicks`, and come back to the entry price again, the trade is done. Distinct from `BreakevenStop`, which arms on a dollar peak profit and exits on the FIRST return, and from `BreakevenRetestExit`, the unarmed first touch — this one cannot fire before the third leg of a touch-recover-touch sequence. THE FILL BAR'S TOUCH DOES NOT COUNT: the fill is at the bar's open, so `Low <= EntryPrice` holds on the entry bar by construction (`Low <= Open` always), and an ungated rule would count it as the first test and degenerate to `BreakevenStop`; the touch test is gated on `BarsSinceEntry >= 1`. The two flags are per position, reset on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED: 0 on the fill bar, restarting through a reversal), never on `MarketPosition = 0`. ORDER IS PART OF THE SPECIFICATION — the recovery is recorded only once a touch is on the books, the exit flag is computed before this bar's touch is recorded, and a single bar spanning both the entry price and `entry + awayTicks` records the touch only. V15-13's tick offset wins over T18-24's "new N-bar high" as the recovery test: no `Highest` call site, and it measures what Brooks is describing, distance from the stop. The `MarketPosition` guard is load-bearing: `EntryPrice` is 0.0 when flat and a back-adjusted price can be negative |
