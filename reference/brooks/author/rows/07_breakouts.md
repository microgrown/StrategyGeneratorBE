# Slice 07 — breakouts_signal_strength

Rows for `rules/CATALOG.md`, one per rule written.

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AvgBodyBelowRangeFraction` | Entry | same | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(3)`, `weakBodyFraction(0.4)` | The mean body of the last N bars is smaller than F × the mean high−low range of the same N bars — a weak, small-bodied and tailed spike. Two fresh current-inclusive `Average`s over price expressions, compared as a product; a ratio of two MEANS, so one doji among big trend bars does not trip it (that is what separates it from `StrongTrendBarRun` negated) and it is not `AverageTailFractionBelow`, which averages per-bar ratios. Not `BodyBelowAtrFraction`, which tests a single body against the ATR |
| `ClosePositionInPriorBarRange` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `threshold(0.5)`, `epsilon(0.000001)` | The close sits in the upper part of the PRIOR bar's range, (close − low[1]) / (high[1] − low[1] + ε) > T; short below 1 − T. A close outside the prior bar's range gives a ratio above 1 or below 0, which `TwoBarRangePosition`'s combined two-bar range can never produce, and `CloseRangePosition` uses the bar's own range. The ε is an input at the corpus's 1e-06, following `CloseRangePosition`: a doji prior bar can have zero range and EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero |
| `ConsecutiveBarsLittleOverlap` | Entry | same | `consecutiveBars` | `consecutiveBars(3)`, `maxOverlapFraction(0.3)` | EVERY one of the last N consecutive bar pairs overlaps by at most F of the SMALLER of the two bars' ranges: overlap = MinList(high[k], high[k+1]) − MaxList(low[k], low[k+1]), divisor = the smaller of high[k] − low[k] and high[k+1] − low[k+1]. The divisor is the whole distinction from `ThinAreaLowBarOverlap`, `OverlappingBarRun` and `MicroChannelSameColorLowOverlap`, which divide the same raw overlap by the EARLIER bar's range — when the newer bar is the smaller one this rule is the stricter test; and it is all-of-N, not `AverageBarOverlap`'s mean. The smaller range is picked with an EL relational rather than MinList, since a bar range is a computed operand and outside MinList's accepted scope; MinList/MaxList take only the tick-grid extremes. A gap gives a negative overlap and passes. The 'little or no pulling back' half of a Brooks spike, with body size left to `StrongTrendBarRun` |
| `FailedFollowThroughCount` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback` | `lookback(6)`, `minCount(3)` | At least K of the last N bars were a bear bar immediately followed by a bull bar (mirrored short) — the opposing side keeps failing to get follow-through. `CountIF` over a TWO-bar pattern, so the counted condition's [1] offsets shift per counted bar, the `TailBarCountDominance` construction; a tie bar closes in neither direction and breaks the pair |
| `LargeRangeBarInWindow` | Entry | same | `window + lookback - 1` | `window(1)`, `rangeMultiple(2)`, `lookback(15)` | At least one of the last W bars had a high−low range of at least K × the mean high−low range of the N bars BEFORE that bar, so a huge bar cannot raise its own benchmark. Negated at W = 1..3 it is Brooks's 'wait one to three bars after a volatility spike'. Not `BarRangeAboveStd` (mean + K standard deviations, current bar only) and not `BarRangeAboveAtrMultiple` / `LargeBarCountInWindow`, which measure against the WFSafe ATR — true range with gaps in, and a current-bar value rather than a per-bar exclusive mean. The shifting benchmark is written as an explicit summation, not `Average(...)[ii + 1]`, because a function-history reference at a loop-variable offset is unregistered |
| `LargeTailedBarCount` | Entry | same | `2 * lookback - 1` | `lookback(8)`, `minCount(4)`, `bodyFractionMax(0.5)` | At least K of the last N bars were BOTH large (range at or above the mean range of the N bars before that bar) and tailed (body no more than F of that bar's range). The AND sits inside the count deliberately — two separate count panes could be satisfied by two different bars. Intended negated: do not trade a breakout of a range full of big tailed bars. Counted with an explicit loop rather than `CountIF` because the large-bar benchmark shifts with the counted bar; the count is the same, bars 0..N−1 current-inclusive. Unlike `TailBarCountDominance` it counts either colour against a threshold and adds the range term |
| `LastTradeWasWinner` | Entry | same | 0 | none | The most recently closed position was profitable, `PositionProfit(1) > 0`, or no trade has closed yet. Brooks: a breakout trade that came out profitable is itself a sign the trend is strong. Unlike `LastTradeCooldown` there is no `BarsSinceExit` leg, so after a loss it never passes until a winner closes — not reachable by any setting of waitAfterWin/waitAfterLoss |
| `NonOppositeClose` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | none | The bar does not close AGAINST the direction: close at or above its open (long), at or below it (short) — Brooks's stated minimum for a follow-through bar to confirm an always-in flip. The non-strict comparison is the entire content: a doji with close = open passes both sides, where `ConsecutiveUpBars` at 1 and `StrongTrendBar` at `lag = 0` (with `bodyFraction = 0`, `tailFraction = 1`) both fail it, so it is not either of those at a parameter setting. The `SignalBarBodyMatchesTradeDirection` key this row originally named was dropped by the dedup pass as `StrongTrendBar` with a lag and was never authored |
| `OppositeLargeBarPair` | Entry | same | `atrLookback + 1` | `bigBarMultiple(1.5)`, `atrLookback(14)` | The last two bars are ADJACENT, both at least K × the ATR read at their own bar, and closed in opposite directions — big up plus big down is a trading range. ATR is MW's `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` rolling form, called once in the precondition; the prior bar's reading comes from the EL variable's own zero-padded history, so it is 0 on the first evaluated bar. Written as two explicit direction branches rather than a boolean not-equal, so a doji has no direction and fails. Adjacency is the claim, which is what keeps it apart from `OppositeLargeBarsInWindow`; expected use is negated, as a trend-continuation gate |
| `OppositeLargeBarsInWindow` | Entry | same | `withinBars + lookback - 1` | `withinBars(5)`, `sizeMultiple(1.5)`, `lookback(20)` | Within the last W bars there is at least one LARGE bull trend bar and at least one LARGE bear trend bar — two spikes in opposite directions close together, which usually resolves sideways. 'Large' is range ≥ K × the mean high−low range of the N bars before that bar, not an ATR, so the rule needs no second WFSafe call site alongside `OppositeLargeBarPair` and gaps stay out of the benchmark. Both directions are required, so identical both sides; a doji counts for neither |

## Not written

- `BarOpensAbovePriorClose` — duplicate of `BodyGapBar` (slice 08, breakouts_volume_gap). Same pseudocode character for character (`open[0] > close[1]`), same `mirrored` sides, both zero-input. Slice 08 should author it; nothing in slice 07 differentiates the two.
- `BodyAboveAverageBody` — suspected duplicate of `BodyAboveAverageMultiple` (slice 04, bar_anatomy_trend_doji), which the merge pass did not cross-reference. That rule is `close[0] > open[0] && (close[0] - open[0]) >= bodyMultiple * average(abs(close - open), bodyLookback)` with `bodyMultiple(1.0)`, `bodyLookback(10)`; at this rule's default `consecutiveBars = 1` and that rule's default `bodyMultiple = 1.0` the two differ only in whether the baseline window includes the bar being tested (this one takes the average at offset k+1, exclusive; slice 04's is current-inclusive). Recommend one rule carrying both `consecutiveBars` and `bodyMultiple`, with the inclusive-versus-exclusive baseline settled once, rather than two rules that agree at their defaults.
