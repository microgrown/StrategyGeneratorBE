T00-47's winning form is `CountIF(dailyClose > dailyOpen AND body >= f × range, N) >= K`; the engine is single-timeframe, so on the M1440 series the corpus already runs, "daily bars" ARE the bars, and that is `StrongBodyBarCountInWindow` with `windowBars = dayLookback`, `minCount = minStrongDays`, `strongBodyFraction = strongBodyFraction` — same three degrees of freedom, mirrored, current-inclusive. The only non-duplicate reading would be session AGGREGATION on an intraday series, which is not what the merge chose. **Grid note: T00-47's defaults are 5 / 2 / 0.6 and R02-12's are 10 / 2, so `StrongBodyBarCountInWindow` should carry `windowBars 5, minCount 2` as a design point** alongside slice 05's 10-of-6 reading.
- `DeepPullbackMeansRange` — SUSPECTED DUPLICATE of `PullbackDepthFromRecentHigh` (slice 13, on disk), for the orchestrator to settle; not authored, and the rest of the slice was. Both are "the pullback has reached fraction F of the leg", with the leg taken as nested `Highest`/`Lowest` windows and written multiplied. The merged form differs only in measuring the pullback to `lowest(low, pullbackLookback)` instead of to `low[0]`, so at `pullbackLookback = 1` it IS the existing rule at `lookback = legLookback`, `retracementFraction = maxPullbackFraction`. The real difference is level-versus-event: the extra window makes the filter STAY true for up to `pullbackLookback` bars after the deep low, where the existing rule fires only on the bar that makes it — the same distinction the corpus draws between `HighLowBarCount` and `LegCountAtLeast`. The merge note itself says "the roll-up should compare the two and keep one". If the standing filter behaviour is wanted, the cheapest fix is a `pullbackLookback(1)` input added to `PullbackDepthFromRecentHigh` rather than a second rule.

## Notes for the roll-up

- Four rules here inline the always-in state machine with `consecutiveBars(2)`/`bodyFraction(0.6)`/`tailFraction(0.2)` exactly as `TrendDurationBars` does, and use the FLIP as the trend-leg reset: `MaxPullbackDepthBelowThreshold`, `PullbackDepthExceedsPriorMax`, `FixedTickPullbackEntry`, `NoQualifyingPullbackSinceLegStart`. That is the settled decision applied, not re-deliberated, and it is what makes "since the trend leg began" concrete for rules whose merged text only said "reset when the trend flag resets". If the always-in definition ever moves, these four move with it and with `TrendDurationBars`.
- Three of those four share one give-back tracker in three readings — the MAXIMUM completed pullback, the LIVE pullback against that maximum, and the running SUM/COUNT for an average. They are written as three independent copies rather than one, because a pane can only AND whole rules; if a fifth reading appears it should be added to this family rather than to a new one.
- Four rules inline `AverageDailyRange` (`TradingRangeDay`, `TrendResumptionDayContext`, `MaxPullbackDepthBelowThreshold`) or the session opening-range block (`TrendDayIdentifiedInFirstHour`), and three more inline the plain session reset. `maxScanBars(500)` is promoted wherever the ADR block appears, per `OpeningRangeVsAvgDailyRange`'s precedent, and Max Bars Back must cover `maxScanBars + 1` for those rules. On a daily or 1440-minute series set `maxScanBars` to `dayLookback + 1`.
- Three rules in this slice are session-scoped day-type reads that were deliberately kept apart and WILL fire on overlapping bars: `SessionTrendDayDirection` (close vs session open, in ATRs, live), `TrendDayIdentifiedInFirstHour` (close vs opening range, in opening-range heights) and `MorningLegDirection` (already on disk, close vs session open, latched at one bar). They should be measured against each other before more than one goes into a template.
- `usePointsThreshold` on `MaxPullbackDepthBelowThreshold` is the only input in this slice that is not a merged parameter. It is a pin-at-0 switch, so it costs nothing in the grid, and it exists so the merged `maxPullbackPoints(3)` default could be preserved without making an instrument-specific point threshold the default behaviour.
