Under the standing signal-bar-close / next-bar-open convention the bar Brooks means — the climax bar being faded — IS the signal bar, so the existing rule already carries the book's intent, and the two differ only in which of two adjacent bars supplies the distance. ## Flagged, authored anyway

- `ClimaxBar` and `LargestTrendBarOfTheRun` are each a sibling-slice rule ANDed with a single strong trend bar — `BarRangeAtrMultiple` (slice 08) and `BarRangeHighestOfWindow` (slice 04) respectively, plus `StrongTrendBarRun` (slice 08) at `consecutiveBars = 1`. The panes AND entries, so both are in principle assemblable from two placements. They are written whole because they are the corpus's named climax classifications, `ConsecutiveClimaxBarCount` counts `ClimaxBar`'s test inline, and the sibling range rules are direction-agnostic while these are mirrored. If the orchestrator would rather have the compositions, these two are the ones to drop.
- `HigherLowBar` is `MicroChannelRunLength` (slice 12, now on disk) at `minBars = 1`: that rule's breaking test is strict (`low[i] < low[i + 1]` clears the flag), so it fires on `low[0] >= low[1]` where this rule needs `low[0] > low[1]`. The two differ only on an equal low. Kept on the corpus's own precedent that a strict/inclusive boundary earns its own rule (`InsideBarInclusive`, `TwoBarRangePosition` versus `CloseRangePosition`); if the orchestrator would rather not carry the tie distinction, this is the cheapest rule in the slice to drop.
- `HigherLowCount` checked against `MicroChannelRunLength` (slice 12) and kept: that rule is all-of-N CONSECUTIVE pairs counted back from the current bar with an INCLUSIVE test, this one is K-of-N pairs anywhere in the window with a STRICT test and tolerates contrary bars, which is the whole point of the count. They coincide only at `minCount = lookback = minBars`, and even there they disagree on ties. Same reasoning the merge pass used to keep `HigherLowCount` apart from `TrendingHighsAndLows`.
