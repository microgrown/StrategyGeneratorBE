## Not written

- `SessionFirstBarBreakout` — **suspected duplicate of `SessionBarBreakout` (B04), stopped for a decision** as the orchestrator asked. Both take the same level (the high/low of the session's FIRST bar) and the same price test (`close[0]` beyond it); they differ only in the quantifier on the bar index. `SessionBarBreakout` requires the bar to be EXACTLY `offsetBars + 1` into the session, which makes it a once-a-day test; `SessionFirstBarBreakout` is a WINDOW, bars 1..`maxBarsIntoSession` after the first bar, and can therefore fire several times in one session. They coincide exactly at `maxBarsIntoSession = 1` / `offsetBars = 1`, which is the skill's third duplicate test ("an existing rule at a fixed parameter"), and neither generalizes the other — `SessionBarBreakout` at `offsetBars = 2` is not reachable from the window form. Brian's call, and it is a cheap one either way: adding a `windowBars(1)` input to `SessionBarBreakout` (fire when the session bar index is between `offsetBars + 1` and `offsetBars + windowBars`) subsumes both in one file, which is the same move that rule's own row already made in collapsing "second bar" and "third bar" into `offsetBars`. If instead the window form is wanted separately it needs a name that says so. B04's author flagged the pair in the same terms and left the decision here.
- `LargeOpeningRangeBreakoutFilter` — **duplicate of `OpeningRangeVsAvgDailyRange` (B07, `time_session`)**, by construction rather than by resemblance. That rule is "the session's opening-range height, as a fraction of the recent average daily range, lies between `lowFraction` and `highFraction`". At `lowFraction = 0` it reads `openingRange <= highFraction * avgDailyRange`, and NEGATED — a per-placement toggle — that is this candidate exactly, with `highFraction = weakBreakoutFraction`. Both a fixed-parameter match and a negation match, the second and third of the skill's three tests. Nothing is lost: the B07 rule carries the small-opening-range reading Brooks states alongside this one, and a placement negated at `highFraction = 0.3` gives this filter's blocking sense. Its `dayLookback(20)` is that rule's average-daily-range window, which B07 authors as the `AverageDailyRange` primitive.
- `QuietPeriodBreakoutNeedsPullback` — **split, not refused**; the quiet half is written above as `QuietBarCountInWindow` and the key does not appear as a file. Per the skill's split rule the merged rule is a top-level conjunction of two independently meaningful conditions — a long quiet run, and an unusually large current bar — and the second is already in the corpus as `BarRangeAtrMultiple` (`(high - low) >= rangeMultiple * WFSafe_AvgTrueRange(atrLookback)`), which is the merged `breakoutBarMultiple` / `atrLookback` terms verbatim. The panes AND entry rules, so `BarRangeAtrMultiple` at `rangeMultiple = 2.0`, `atrLookback = 14` placed with `QuietBarCountInWindow` at `minQuietBars = 30`, `smallRangeFraction = 0.7`, `lookback = 60`, `atrLookback = 14` reproduces the merged rule bar for bar, and each half can be tuned, negated and reused on its own. The merge's `catalog_match` named `BarRangeAboveStd`, which is the wrong neighbour — that one is a standard-deviation test on the bar's range with no ATR and no window count. The name change is the only thing needing a nod: `B_KEYS.md` carries the compound key and the corpus now carries the atomic one.

## Notes for the roll-up

- Three rules mirror `CloseD(1)` (`SessionGapAtrDirectional`, `SessionGapAtrMagnitude`, `GapHoldsAbovePriorSessionClose`) and three mirror `HighD(1)`/`LowD(1)` (`SessionOpeningGapBeyondPriorExtreme`, `GapPullbackFailureEntry`, `SessionGapFirstBarReversalEntry`). The two mirrors are NOT the same warm-up shape and the difference is deliberate: the prior day's CLOSE is knowable from `close[1]` on the date-change bar alone, so that mirror is exact from its first observed date change, while the prior day's HIGH and LOW need the whole day observed and therefore keep B04's `sawStart` guard and lose one extra day. Both keep the measured `-1` sentinel so the guard means the same thing in both twins. If B07's session model changes, all six follow it.
- `GapPullbackFailureEntry` is the one rule in the slice that needs `OpenD(0)`/`HighD(0)`/`LowD(0)` — the running, current-bar-inclusive session values. That is what removes the bars-into-session counter the merged pseudocode implied, so it is worth keeping if the session primitives are ever factored out.
- `SessionGapAtrMagnitude` and `SessionGapAtrDirectional` are the same measurement signed and unsigned. Neither can be built from the other by a placement toggle (a placement carries one side, and the panes AND rather than OR), which is the reason two files exist; a grid that places both should pin them to the same `atrLength`.
- `TwiceFailedBreakoutAttempt`'s counter is the first in the corpus to count a boolean VARIABLE's own history through `CountIF` with a mirrored ring buffer on the C++ side. `SecondFailedSwingBreakoutReversal` (B04) solved the same problem with an age counter because its event embeds a swing function whose lagged read has no register row; here the event is pure price, so `CountIF` over the plain variable is available and generalizes to any `minFailedAttempts`.
- Gap rules stated on daily bars: all six session-gap rules are marked intraday-only in their rows because their levels are session-scoped. On a daily or 1440-minute series `HighD(1)`/`LowD(1)`/`CloseD(1)` degrade exactly to series indexing (measured, 58/58) and every bar is its own session, so they do not break — they degenerate into bar-to-bar tests that the corpus already carries under other names. `MultiSessionPauseBreakout` is the one rule in the slice whose parameter is honest only on daily/1440 bars, and its row says so.
