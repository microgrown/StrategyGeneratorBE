| `MicroTrendLineFailedBreakout` | Entry | mirrored | `max(pivotLookback - 1 + microSwingStrength, failureWindowBars)` | `microSwingStrength(1)`, `pivotLookback(20)`, `microLineBars(10)`, `failureWindowBars(2)` | A bar within the last `failureWindowBars` poked THROUGH the micro trend line — for a long, a bar whose low traded below the bull micro line — and this bar closes back through that bar's own opposite extreme. | `TrendLineTouch` | Entry | mirrored | `max(pivotLookback - 1 + swingStrength, atrLength + 1)` | `swingStrength(2)`, `pivotLookback(20)`, `touchTolAtr(0.1)`, `atrLength(14)` | In a bull trend the bar dips to — or slightly through — the projected BULL trend line (the one across the two most recent confirmed swing lows) and closes back above it; the short side is the mirror at the bear line. The with-trend pullback buy at the line, and the base rule of the trend-line family: T13-03 and T15-13 are the same setup written twice, T21-20 the same test inside a spike-and-channel, R10-02 the proximity half alone ("trend lines are magnets"), R28-38 a limit order AT the line, which under the standing signal-bar-close / next-bar-open convention mechanizes to the same bar. ## Not written

- `ChannelHeightEqualsSpikeHeightTarget` — duplicate of `PatternHeightMeasuredMoveTarget` (`multiple = 1`), which the corpus already has. The merge notes anticipated exactly this ("the arithmetic is identical to the spike measured-move target R07-02 ... the authoring pass MUST check R07-02's merged rule first and author only one of the two"), and R07-02's rule is the one already on disk. `PatternHeightMeasuredMoveTarget` takes the high−low span of the `refBars` bars that produced the breakout and projects a multiple of it beyond the broken extreme; when `refBars` is the spike's length the window's extreme IS the spike's end and the span IS the spike's height, so the two compute the same price. Nothing in R07-05's channel framing ("the height of the channel is often about the same as the height of the spike") changes the arithmetic — it is an argument for `multiple = 1`, not a second rule. The merged `toleranceAtr` is the only difference and it is the existing rule's `tolerancePoints` in ATR units.
- `ChannelStartRetestExit` — duplicate either way it is constructed, and both targets already exist. Under this family's channel construction the channel start is `SwingLow` Occurrence 2 for a bear-direction target and `SwingHigh` Occurrence 2 for a bull-direction one, which is exactly `SwingPivotTarget` at `pivotIndex = 2` — a use that rule's own comment names ("R05-21 targets the top of the wedge's SECOND push, pivotIndex 2"). Under the merged pseudocode's Donchian reading (`channelStart = lowest(low, pullbackBars)`) it is `ExitAtPriorRangeExtreme`, which is `Lowest(Low, channelBars)` with a tolerance. The merged `toleranceAtr` versus those rules' `tolerancePoints` is a units choice, not a behavioural difference. Seven sources agree on the LEVEL, and the level is already reachable; nothing is lost. If Brian wants R05-22's shallower anchor (the spike TOP rather than the pullback low) that is `SwingPivotTarget` at `pivotIndex = 1`.
- `ThreePushesInChannel` — duplicate of `ThreePushPattern` (slice B03, on disk), at `shapeMode = 0`. `ThreePushPattern`'s short side is `patUp`, three successively higher confirmed swing highs inside `lookbackBars`, which is this rule's condition verbatim; its long side is the mirror. The merge notes predicted the collision and named the deciding test ("the roll-up should reconcile against three_push_patterns' version, and if that one carries the anchor as a parameter this becomes a duplicate") — `lookbackBars(40)` is that anchor as a parameter, bounding how far back the three pushes may reach, which is all "since the channel began" contributes once the channel's start is not separately identified. T21-25's "most three-push patterns function exactly like wedges regardless of shape" is `shapeMode` 0 versus 1, already an input there.
- `TrendChannelLineOvershoot` — suspected duplicate the merge pass could not have seen, of `TrendLineTouch` in this same slice, at a NEGATIVE `touchTolAtr`. Its merged pseudocode is the PARALLEL construction (T14-03: the trend line's parallel, anchored at the extreme pivot between the trend line's own two anchors); per the slice brief the parallel reading was NOT carried, and B01's construction was substituted. Under that substitution the long side becomes `low[0] < loNow − overshootAtr × ATR and close[0] > loNow` — reading the line through the two most recent swing LOWS, which is the SAME line `TrendLineTouch`'s long side reads — and `TrendLineTouch` at `touchTolAtr = −overshootAtr` is that condition exactly. The two are not merely overlapping: one is the other at a fixed parameter, which is `author-rule` §2's third test. `TrendLineTouch`'s row records the negative-band reading so the trade is not lost, and R26-05's reversal-bar half is a `bar_anatomy_reversal_signal` rule ANDed in the pane, as its merge notes already direct. If Brian decides the corpus should carry the PARALLEL channel line after all, this key becomes a real rule again and the whole channel family has to be revisited with it — that is one decision, not one rule.
- `TrendLineSlopeFlattening` — blocked on `swinghigh` / `swinglow` **Occurrence 3**. The construction decision that downgraded it from research to hard ("the current line is always the newest confirmed pivot pair, so the prior line is the pair one pivot back") makes the comparison three pivots wide by definition: `swingLowBar(1)`, `(2)` and `(3)`, with their prices. The VERIFIED register rows list Occurrence 3+ explicitly as unmeasured, and B01 blocked `StairsPattern` and `ShrinkingStairs` on the same clause. Nothing else in the rule is a problem — two slopes and a fraction. NOTE FOR THE ORCHESTRATOR: slice B03 took a different route on the identical blocker. `ThreePushPattern` and its family need three pivots and get them by INLINING the pivot scan in BOTH twins (an explicit EL `for` loop over `High[jj]`/`Low[jj]`, no `SwingHigh` call at all), which uses no unmeasured word and keeps the twins statement for statement. That route unblocks this rule as written and needs no probe; it was not taken here because the slice brief says rules needing Occurrence 3 or more are blocked, and choosing between the two routes is a corpus-wide decision rather than one slice's. The other route is the probe: extending `EL_Swing_Probe.txt` to Occurrence 3 and 4 unblocks this rule, `StairsPattern` and `ShrinkingStairs` together.

## Notes for the orchestrator

- **The channel/trend-line construction from B01 was used unchanged, and the parallel reading was NOT carried.** Bull trend line through the two most recent confirmed swing LOWS, bull channel line through the two most recent swing HIGHS, bear mirrored; every rule adds `pivotLookback(20)`, guards the `-1` sentinel and `Bar1 <> Bar2`, and uses `TLValue`'s older-anchor-first form. The consequence this slice found: under one non-parallel construction the "trend line" and the "channel line" of the opposite-direction channel ARE THE SAME LINE, so a trend-line rule and a channel-line rule on opposite sides can collapse onto each other. That is what removed `TrendChannelLineOvershoot`, and it is the reason `TrendLineTouch`'s tolerance is documented as signed rather than a second rule being written. Anyone reversing the B01 decision must revisit that row and this Not-written entry together.
- **Two keys in later slices are already covered by rows here and should not be authored separately.** B09's `TrendChannelLineTarget` ("the parallel of the trend line through the swing lows, anchored at the intervening swing high") is `ChannelLineTargetExit` once the parallel is replaced by B01's construction — same line, same test, same role. B10's `CountertrendNeedsPriorTrendLineBreak` ("a countertrend entry is permitted only if, within the last N bars, price has already closed through the trend line") is `TrendLineBrokenRecently` verbatim, including the window.
- **Input naming is the merged names, which are not uniform across the two families.** The channel rules spell the pivot strength `pivotStrength` and the trend-line rules `swingStrength` (and `microSwingStrength` in the micro rule); they mean the same thing and take the same default. `pivotLookback(20)` was added to all nine, since the merged parameters omitted it and it is EL's `Length` — it bounds how old an anchor may be and is load-bearing.
- **Both `pivotStrength`/`swingStrength` and `pivotLookback` should be PINNED in grids**, not optimized: they change which pivots exist and so move every line discontinuously. The ATR multiples, the window lengths and the counts are the tunable inputs. `touchTolAtr` on `TrendLineTouch` is the one input whose grid should be allowed to go NEGATIVE.
- **Nothing leans on an `ASSUMED` register row and nothing needed an unregistered word.** Words used: `swinghigh`, `swinglow`, `swinghighbar`, `swinglowbar`, `tlvalue`, `wfsafe_avgtruerange`, `absvalue`, `marketposition`, `date`, `high`, `low`, `close`, all VERIFIED or ACCEPTED. No `ema`/`xaverage` read anywhere, so the unrun lag probe does not touch this slice. `highest`/`lowest` were deliberately NOT used in `ChannelBounceFractionTarget`: its window length is a pivot's bars-ago count, and a variable in EL's `Length` slot is unmeasured, so the extreme is an explicit loop written the same way in both twins.
- **Outside the EL translation table: nothing.** No `classMembersHook`, no `std::` containers, no ternaries in any condition. The only C++-only constructs are loop-local `int`/`bool` scratch inside the hooks and `std::fabs` for `AbsValue`, both as `TightChannel` and `BreakoutLevelRetest` already do. `FirstTrendLineBreakOfSession` carries the only cross-bar STATE in the slice — two session latches, mirroring EL variables, reset on `date <> date[1]`.
- **One simplification is shared by three rules and is worth Brian's eye.** `TrendLineBrokenRecently`, `TrendLineTouchCount` and `SecondTrendLineTest` all evaluate the line at older bars of a window using the pivots AS THEY STAND NOW, not as they stood at that bar. Re-deriving the pivot set per bar is not expressible in EL (`SwingLowBar` takes no offset), B01 made the same call in `ChannelLineBreakoutFailure`, and both twins do it identically so the comparison against MultiWalk still holds — but the counted history is a redrawn line, not the line a trader saw at the time.
