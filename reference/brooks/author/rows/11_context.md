# CATALOG rows — slice 11_context (context / regime: trading-range width, bar overlap, barbwire, trend strength)

Sixteen rules in the slice, eleven written with both twins, five not written.
Rows are in `rules/CATALOG.md` table format, ready to append.

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple` | Entry | same | `max(rangeLookback - 1, atrLookback + 1)` | `rangeLookback(20)`, `rangeAtrMultiple(4)`, `atrLookback(14)` | The height of the last N bars (highest high − lowest low) is no more than K × the N-bar ATR — the corpus's canonical trading-range classification. Loose (20 bars, 4.0) reads "in a trading range"; tight (15 bars, ~1.2) reads "tight trading range"; negate the placement for "trending". ATR is MW's rolling `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange`, not a fresh mean. Not `AdxBelowThreshold`: ADX can sit low inside a slow one-sided drift that this rule calls wide |
| `TightRangeBlocksEntries` | Switch | same | `max(rangeLookback - 1, atrLookback + 1)` | `rangeLookback(15)`, `tightAtrMultiple(1.2)`, `atrLookback(20)` | The last N bars are tighter than K × the N-bar ATR — Brooks's "a tight trading range trumps everything", as a global override rather than a per-pane filter. Same arithmetic as `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple` at tight defaults, kept apart by ROLE only (the `ConsecutiveCloseExit` / `MomentumConsecutiveBars` precedent). CAVEAT: the generator's Switch also forces flat, and Brooks wants only the entry block — see "Divergences" below |
| `OverlappingBarRun` | Entry | same | `overlapBars` | `overlapBars(4)`, `overlapFraction(0.5)` | Each of the last N bar pairs overlaps by at least fraction F of the OLDER bar's range: `MinList(High, High[1]) − MaxList(Low, Low[1]) >= F × (High[1] − Low[1])`, counted with `CountIF` over the window. Brooks's bar-overlap reading of sideways, two-sided trading. A gap simply fails the pair (negative overlap, non-negative threshold), so no clamp. At `overlapBars = 1` it degenerates to `BarOverlapFraction`; the run over N pairs is what it adds |
| `Barbwire` | Entry | same | 2 | `barbwireOverlapFraction(0.5)`, `dojiBodyFraction(0.2)` | Brooks's named barbwire, mechanized per R22-03: more than fraction F of the MIDDLE of the last three bars lies inside the block its two neighbours span, AND at least one of the three is a doji (body ≤ `dojiBodyFraction` × its own range, the same threshold `DojiBar` uses). Carried as a composite deliberately — barbwire is a named setup Brian wants tested as the book states it |
| `RangeMidpointZone` | Entry | same | `rangeLookback - 1` | `rangeLookback(20)`, `midpointFraction(0.17)` | The close sits within F of the range height either side of the N-bar range's midpoint — at 0.17 the middle third. Brooks uses it NEGATED: no stop entry in the middle of a range, where the fill is bad in both directions. Not the negation of a range-extreme fade, which on the long side still admits the top of the range |
| `BarsSinceExtremeExceeds` | Entry | mirrored | `rangeBars + priorLookback - 1` | `rangeBars(10)`, `priorLookback(10)` | The highest high of the last N bars is strictly below the highest high of the longer N+M window behind it (short: lowest low above) — no new extreme for N bars, Brooks's test for a pullback that has outgrown its trend and become a trading range. Stateless two-window form, no spike detector. Not `CloseAbovePriorHighestHigh`: neither window here reads the close |
| `EntryBarClosedAgainstPosition` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | — | A position is open and this bar closed against it: long side fires when short and the bar closed up, short side when long and it closed down. An Entry that reads position state deliberately, like `FlatOrOpenLossExceeds` — but on the sign of the bar, so it can fire on the first bar of the trade before any loss has accrued |
| `FailedCountertrendScalpReversal` | Entry | mirrored | 2 | — | A three-bar trap: bar 2 was a bear bar (`Close[2] < Open[2]`), bar 1 triggered the short by trading below it (`Low[1] < Low[2]`), and bar 0 closes back above bar 2's high, where the trapped shorts' stops sit. Mirrored for the bull-signal-bar trap |
| `BlockEntriesAfterLosingMonth` | Entry | same | 0 | — | The PRIOR completed calendar month's closed-trade result was not negative — an entry gate that stands down for a month after a losing one. Latched on the first bar of each new month from `NetProfit` alone (closed trades), so it never moves inside the month. | `RangeMidpointTarget` | Exit | mirrored | `rangeLookback` | `rangeLookback(40)`, `toleranceFraction(0.01)` | Price reaches the midpoint of the N-bar range ENDING ONE BAR AGO — the magnet a failed breakout is pulled back to. Long exits when the high reaches `mid − toleranceFraction × rangeHeight`, short when the low reaches `mid +` it. The book's one-tick tolerance is expressed as a fraction of the range height, because ticks need the unregistered `MinMove`/`PriceScale` |
| `CountertrendScalpPercentTarget` | Exit | mirrored | 0 | `scalpPercent(0.015)` | Close reaches `EntryPrice + scalpPercent × \|EntryPrice\|` (short mirrored below) — a percentage-of-price scalp target rather than `TakeProfit`'s fixed dollars, which is the point on weekly and monthly charts. The absolute value is a guard for back-adjusted continuous futures, where a negative entry price would otherwise invert the comparison. Book values 1% and 2%; 1.5% is the midpoint default |

## Not written

- `BounceFromLowByAtr` — suspected duplicate of `DistanceAboveRecentLow` (slice 13, swing_structure), which A_KEYS describes as the same test: close risen at least K × ATR above the lowest low of the last N bars, mirrored on the high. Neither file exists yet; one key should survive.
- `RangeExtremeFade` — suspected duplicate of `CloseInRangePosition` (slice 02, bar_anatomy_reversal_signal): "the close sits in the bottom (long) / top (short) fraction of the N-bar high-low range", which is this rule's whole content. Neither file exists yet.
- `ConsecutiveTrendBarRun` — suspected duplicate of `ConsecutiveTrendBars` (slice 04, bar_anatomy_trend_doji): each of the last N bars a trend bar in the same direction, body at least a fraction of its own range. Same condition, same two inputs. Also a subset of `StrongTrendBarRun` (slice 08), which adds tail tests.
- `StrongTrendBarCount` — suspected duplicate of `TrendBarCountInWindow` (slice 05) and of `StrongBodyBarCountInWindow` (slice 04): at least K of the last N bars a with-trend trend bar. The count-over-window form with the same three degrees of freedom.
- `NoOppositeBarInLastN` — suspected duplicate by fixed parameter of `OppositeBodyCountInWindow` (slice 12, lines_channels_channels): "the number of bars in the last N whose body is against the trend direction is at or below K" is this rule exactly at K = 0, and the count form is strictly more general. Its doji tolerance versus `ConsecutiveUpBars` (the merge pass's reason for keeping it) is preserved by that rule too.

## Divergences and conventions

- **Switch cannot block without forcing flat.** `strategyWriter.py:284-295` emits a Switch as `enterX = enterX && !(cond)` AND `exitX = exitX || (cond)`, so every Switch liquidates. The merge pass wanted `TightRangeBlocksEntries` to block entries only ("wait for the breakout"). It is written as a Switch anyway, because the role is the only thing separating it from `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple`, and the divergence is recorded in the rule's own comment. A block-only Switch role in the generator would remove the caveat.
- **Tick units.** `RangeMidpointTarget`'s merged `toleranceTicks × tickSize` became `toleranceFraction` of the range height (0.01). No other rule in the slice reached for ticks.
- **`CountertrendScalpPercentTarget` sides.** The slice says `same`; written `mirrored`, because an Exit's long condition closes longs and the target has to sit above the entry price on one side and below it on the other.
- **`maxlist` / `minlist`** are used only for selection among raw highs and lows — exactly the tick-grid-price scope the register's ACCEPTED row allows — and the C++ stand-ins are plain comparisons for that reason.
- **`WFSafe_AvgTrueRange`** in the two width rules is reproduced in the rolling, two-statement, re-seeding form from `AtrProfitTarget`, in `preConditionHook` so it advances on every bar as EL's non-short-circuiting evaluation does.
- Every scratch value in a hook is a declared local rather than a hook-scope `double`, so a second placement of the same rule gets its own prefixed copy.
