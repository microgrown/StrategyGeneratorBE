# CATALOG rows — slice 12, lines and channels (channels)

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AverageBarOverlap` | Entry | same | `lookback` | `lookback(10)`, `maxAvgOverlap(0.5)`, `epsilon(0.000001)` | The MEAN pairwise bar overlap over the last N pairs is at or below T: each pair contributes `(MinList(High[k], High[k+1]) − MaxList(Low[k], Low[k+1]))`, clamped to 0 on a gap, divided by the OLDER bar's range `High[k+1] − Low[k+1]`. Low mean overlap is a strong channel or spike; place negated for the two-sided-channel sense. A MEAN, so one bad pair does not veto it — that is what separates it from the all-pairs tests (`ConsecutiveBarsLittleOverlap`, `ThinAreaLowBarOverlap`, `OverlappingBarRun`) and from `BarOverlapFraction`'s single pair. The ε guards a zero-range reference bar (EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero on a zero denominator); the gap clamp is an EL `If ov < 0 Then ov = 0`, not `MaxList(0, ov)`, since 0 against a price DIFFERENCE is a computed operand outside MinList/MaxList's ACCEPTED scope |
| `CountertrendRunInWindow` | Entry | mirrored | `windowBars` | `windowBars(20)`, `swingBars(5)` | Somewhere in the last N bars there was a run of K consecutive bars each making a strictly lower high than the bar before it (long) / a strictly higher low (short) — a broad countertrend swing inside a channel, which Brooks reads as "tradable in both directions". Every candidate start in the window is scanned, so the run need not end on the current bar; that is the difference from `ConsecutiveUpBars`, which also counts a different per-bar test (close vs its own open). Measures the LENGTH of the countertrend run, not its depth, so it is not `TightChannel` negated |
| `MicroChannelBreakoutBar` | Entry | mirrored | `minBars + 1` | `minBars(5)` | The first bar to violate a confirmed micro channel: none of the `minBars` bar pairs ENDING AT BAR 1 broke it (no lower low, inclusive) and this bar makes a strictly lower low; short is the mirror on highs. Fires on the bar the run ENDS, where `MicroChannelRunLength` fires while it holds — the two never fire on the same bar, which is why both exist |
| `MicroChannelFailedBreakoutEntry` | Entry | mirrored | `maxWaitBars + minBars + 1` | `minBars(5)`, `maxWaitBars(3)` | Some bar k in the last `maxWaitBars` bars was a micro-channel breakout bar (the channel held over the `minBars` pairs ending at bar k+1, and bar k made a strictly lower low) and this bar closes back above `High[k]` — the failed breakout that resumes the channel's direction; short is the mirror. The breakout bar is found by a bounded backward scan rather than persisted state, so the rule carries no history |
| `MicroChannelRunLength` | Entry | mirrored | `minBars` | `minBars(5)` | None of the last `minBars` bar pairs made a lower low (long) / a higher high (short) — Brooks's micro channel. A run counted back from the current bar reaches `minBars` exactly when the bounded all-pairs test holds, so the counter needs no persisted state. Containment is INCLUSIVE (an equal low keeps a bull micro channel alive), which is the boundary that separates it from the strict `HigherLowCount` at `minCount = lookback`; `LaggedHigherHigh` is this rule's short side negated only at `minBars = 1`, and the rule of interest is the run at 4–10 (10 being T16-07's climax setting) |
| `OppositeBodyCountInWindow` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(10)`, `maxOpposite(3)` | At most K of the last N bars closed against the direction — `CountIF(Close < Open, lookback) <= maxOpposite` long, the mirror short. Few bear bodies in a bull channel means the channel is still one-sided; place negated for the opposite reading (selling pressure building), which is the same count and why the two source senses are one rule. A tie bar counts for neither side. Counts BODIES against a constant, where `TailBarCountDominance` counts TAIL bars and compares the two counts against each other |
| `PullbackFromRecentHigh` | Entry | mirrored | `max(lookback - 1, atrLength + 1)` | `lookback(10)`, `pullbackAtr(0.5)`, `atrLength(14)` | The bar's LOW is at least K × ATR below the highest high of the last N bars (long) / the bar's HIGH at least K × ATR above the lowest low (short) — Brooks's "limit orders at fixed intervals below the most recent high", the first tranche only, in ATR rather than in cents so it ports across instruments. The extreme window INCLUDES the current bar. ATR is `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange`'s rolling accumulator, called once in the hook so exactly one call site advances per bar. Reads the bar's own extreme, not its close, because a limit order is filled intrabar |
| `TightChannel` | Entry | mirrored | `max(windowBars, atrLength + 1)` | `windowBars(10)`, `netAtr(2)`, `pullbackAtr(1)`, `atrLength(14)` | Over the last N bars the market made net progress of at least X × ATR while the deepest pullback from its RUNNING extreme never exceeded Y × ATR — Brooks's tight channel, measured with no trend lines, no swing pivots and no daily-range series. The window is walked oldest first so the running high precedes the low it is measured against; walking newest first would measure a distance to a future high. The T21-30 "and no close below the 20 EMA" term is deliberately left out — it is a separate ema_relations filter that ANDs in the pane. Both halves scale in ATR (`WFSafe_AvgTrueRange`, one call site in the hook) |

Its overlap half is an all-pairs low-overlap run, i.e. the planned `ConsecutiveBarsLittleOverlap` (slice 07) / `ThinAreaLowBarOverlap` (slice 02), differing only in the divisor — this key divides by the OLDER bar's range, slice 07's key by the SMALLER bar's range. ## Notes for the orchestrator

- Slice 01's `AverageBarOverlapWindow` row asks which divisor slice 12 chose. It is the same one slice 01 picked: the PRIOR (older) bar's range, `High[k+1] − Low[k+1]`. So the two really were one rule, and `AverageBarOverlap` as written here is it, read in the sources' low-overlap direction.
- Three-way collision the merge pass missed, all in the same statistic as `OppositeBodyCountInWindow`, none of them yet on disk: `NoOppositeBarInLastN` (slice 11) is this rule at `maxOpposite = 0` — its A_KEYS line says "no bar in the window closed below its own open", which is the body test, not a close-vs-close test; `OppositeCloseCountBelowThreshold` (slice 14) is this rule with `<` in place of `<=`. `OppositeBodyCountInWindow` is the general form and was written; the other two should be dropped unless one of them means close-vs-prior-close, which its description does not say.
- Family overlap around `PullbackFromRecentHigh`, again not yet on disk: `BounceFromLowByAtr` (slice 11) and `DistanceAboveRecentLow` (slice 13) look like each other outright — both are "close has moved K × ATR away from the N-bar extreme", same window, same ATR. `PullbackFromRecentHigh` differs from both in the operand (the bar's own low/high, not its close) and in which side gets which measurement, and the operand difference is load-bearing here because a limit order is filled intrabar. Worth a look while adjudicating 11 vs 13.
- `MicroChannelRunLength` vs `HigherLowCount` (slice 13) is a near-miss worth recording: at `minCount = lookback` the two coincide except that this rule's containment is inclusive (equal lows keep the channel) and `HigherLowCount`'s is strict, and `HigherLowCount` allows non-consecutive pairs. Written on that basis, not stopped, since three rules in this slice key off the micro-channel run.
- `ChannelSlopeSteep` and `DipBelowPriorBarLow` / `ShallowDipBelowPriorBarLow` were named in the dispatch brief but are NOT entries in `slices/12_lines.json`; they appear only in other rules' `variants_kept_separate`. Nothing was authored or blocked for them, and `LinearRegSlope` was never reached for — no rule in this slice needed an unregistered word.
