# Slice 14 — misc + three_push_patterns + volume + leg_counting + time_session + trade_management

CATALOG rows for the eleven rules written. Both twins exist for each;
`validateRule` reports zero errors and zero warnings on all eleven, and
`python lintElFeatures.py` is clean (0 blocking, 0 warnings).

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AverageRangeBelowDollarThreshold` | Entry | same | `rangeLookback - 1` | `minRangeDollars(1)`, `rangeLookback(20)` | The mean bar range `Average(High − Low, N)` — window INCLUSIVE of the current bar, a fresh EL `Average` over a price expression — is below the threshold. Brooks's VP3-14 "under a dollar of daily range, so there is no scalp in it". The threshold is in PRICE units: there is deliberately no `BigPointValue` factor, so it reads as dollars only on a $1-per-point instrument (the book's stock). Not `AtrDollarsBelow`, which is the `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` rolling accumulator, true range rather than range, and scaled to dollars; not `NarrowRangeBar`, which sizes THIS bar against the same mean instead of thresholding the mean itself |
| `DeepPullbackRetracementFilter` | Entry | mirrored | `legLookback` | `legLookback(30)`, `retracementThreshold(0.75)` | The close has retraced at least fraction R back across the leg: `close − legLow ≥ R × (legHigh − legLow)` long, `legHigh − close ≥ R × (...)` short, with the leg taken as the highest high and lowest low of the N bars ENDING ONE BAR AGO (EL `Highest(High, N)[1]`, so the current bar is out of its own benchmark). Brooks's "a 75% retracement flips the trend bias" — the bear leg's pullback is now a new bull trend. Written as a multiplication, so a zero-height leg cannot divide by zero. Not `PullbackDepthFromRecentHigh`, which measures the bar's LOW down from the leg high over a current-inclusive window and assigns the sides the other way round; not `DistanceAboveRecentLow`, which measures the same close-above-the-low distance against K × ATR instead of a fraction of the leg. The leg is the fixed window rather than a swing-pivot search — `SwingHigh`/`SwingLow` are unregistered |
| `TrendingBarBodies` | Entry | mirrored | `trendBars` | `trendBars(3)` | Every one of the last N bar pairs has BOTH a higher body top and a higher body bottom (short: both lower): `MaxList(Open, Close)` and `MinList(Open, Close)` compared pair by pair. Brooks's V02-26 "trending anything: closes, highs, lows, or bodies", the bodies reading. All-of-N, written as `TrendingHighsAndLows` writes it — start true, let any failing pair clear the flag — so a tie on either body edge fails both sides. Kept apart from `TrendingCloseRun` (not yet on disk) and from `TrendingHighsAndLows`: a bar can trend its close or its extremes without trending its body. `MaxList`/`MinList` select among raw opens and closes, tick-grid prices, which is the scope `EL_FEATURES` accepts them for |
| `AdverseBodyCountExit` | Exit | mirrored | `adverseBars - 1` | `adverseBars(2)` | Each of the last N bars closed below its own open (exit the long) / above its own open (exit the short) — `CountIF(Close < Open, N) ≥ N`, the source's `= N` written without a float equality since the count can never exceed N. Brooks's VP1-25 tighter-than-a-stop bail-out for a trade taken on a weak signal. Reads no position state: an exit BY PLACEMENT, the streak may pre-date the entry — the `ConsecutiveCloseExit` precedent. Same condition as `ConsecutiveUpBars` mirrored, in the Exit pane, which an Entry rule cannot occupy. A tie bar (close equal to open) breaks both streaks |
| `OutsideOutsidePattern` | Entry | same | 2 | — | Brooks's "oo": the bar two back is engulfed by the bar one back, which is itself engulfed by this bar — `High[1] > High[2] AND Low[1] < Low[2] AND High > High[1] AND Low < Low[1]`, strict on all four as the source writes it. A two-bar expanding triangle; direction comes from the breakout, hence identical both sides. The source's extra `(High − Low) > (High[1] − Low[1])` term is implied by the last two comparisons and dropped. Not `OioPattern` or `IoiPattern` (three bars, alternating inside/outside) and not `LargeOutsideBar` (one bar, sized against the mean range) |
| `ShrinkingConsecutiveRanges` | Entry | same | `2 * flagBars - 1` | `flagBars(8)`, `shrinkFraction(1)` | The last N bars span less than F × the span of the N bars before them: `Highest(High, N) − Lowest(Low, N)` against the same expression offset by `[N]`, i.e. two adjacent fixed-length windows. Brooks's V07-05 two consecutive flags with the second smaller, which sets up a wedge reversal on the next breakout. `shrinkFraction` 1.0 reproduces the book's literal "the second one being smaller" and is the natural thing to optimize downward. Not `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple`, which sizes one window against ATR rather than against its own predecessor |
| `MinimumAbsoluteVolume` | Entry | same | 0 | `minVolume(3000000)` | The bar's TOTAL ticks are at or above an absolute floor — a share or contract count, not a ratio. Brooks's liquidity gate at four scales (3M and 5M shares for daily stock selection, 25,000 and 100,000 Emini contracts intraday); the default is instrument-specific either way. Reads `Ticks` / `ctx.Ticks`, up + down, NOT the `volume` alias: the engine's and EL's `Volume` is UP-TICKS only (`EL_Volume_Probe.txt`, @KW 480min and 1440min, 18,203/18,203 bars), so an absolute count of an up-tick series would not mean what the book means. `VolumeAboveAverageMultiple` makes the same substitution. Identical both sides |
| `VolumeBelowAverageFraction` | Entry | same | `volLookback - 1` | `volumeFraction(0.8)`, `volLookback(20)` | The bar's volume is at or below F × its own N-bar average volume, window INCLUSIVE of the current bar. Brooks's T06-24 "quiet, low-volume market" gate; Brooks says only that volume is low, so the 0.8 is a reading and that is exactly why it is an input. `volume` here is EL's `Volume`, i.e. UP-TICKS, so this is an up-tick RATIO — deliberate, and the same series `VolumeBelowAverage` measures; an absolute floor could not use it, which is why `MinimumAbsoluteVolume` reads `Ticks` instead. `Volume` is a data series with real history, so the average is a fresh `Average` over the series, not the zero-filled variable mirror `VolumeAboveAverageMultiple` needs. `VolumeBelowAverage` is this rule at `volumeFraction` 1.0 with a strict `<` |
| `PauseBarBroad` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `dojiBodyFraction(0.2)` | Brooks's BROAD pullback definition (RP3-02, "any pause should be considered a pullback, even though there may be only sideways action"): the bar is an inside bar, OR it closed against the trend, OR it is a doji. An OR of three independently meaningful conditions, which is the one shape the AND-ing entry panes cannot assemble from parts, so it is deliberately one rule. The inside-bar half uses Brooks's INCLUSIVE boundary at lag 0 (`High ≤ High[1] AND Low ≥ Low[1]`, `InsideBarInclusive`'s convention, not `InsideBar`'s strict one) because the broad reading is the point. `dojiBodyFraction` 0.2 is `DojiBar`'s threshold, the value `Barbwire` already carries. Kept apart from `PullbackBar`, the strict lower-low definition Brooks states as the competing one in the same paragraph |
| `PriorSessionLevelTest` | Entry | mirrored | `atrLookback + 1` | `levelSelect(3)`, `toleranceAtr(0.1)`, `atrLookback(14)` | The bar's low (long, testing the level from below) or high (short, from above) comes within `toleranceAtr × ATR` of one of the prior session's four reference prices, selected by `levelSelect`: 0 = high, 1 = low, 2 = open, 3 = close. Brooks's yesterday's-high/low/open/close magnets as one rule with a selector rather than four near-identical files. ONE BAR IS ONE SESSION: on daily or 1440-minute bars the four prices are `High[1]`/`Low[1]`/`Open[1]`/`Close[1]`, so the rule needs no session model, no `HighD`/`LowD`/`OpenD`/`CloseD` and no −1 warm-up sentinel — on finer intraday bars it reads the prior BAR, which is not what Brooks means, so daily / 1440-minute only. A TOUCH, not a break, which is what separates it from `CloseAbovePriorHigh`. The sources' "1 or 2 ticks" is carried as a fraction of the `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` rolling ATR because `MinMove`/`PriceScale` are UNKNOWN and the engine exposes no tick size — the substitution `PullbackFromRecentHigh` and `DistanceAboveRecentLow` make. One ATR call site, in the hook |
| `FirstTradingDayOfMonthFilter` | Entry | same | 1 (the `Date` series) | `firstOfMonthWindow(1)` | The bar falls within the first N TRADING days of the calendar month: `dayIndex` resets to 1 when `Month(Date) <> Month(Date[1])` and adds one on each later new date, and the rule fires while `1 ≤ dayIndex ≤ firstOfMonthWindow`. R31-12's calendar half — the fraction-of-capital half of the dollar-cost-averaging analogy is a portfolio feature the single-position model does not expose and is dropped. The source's `Day(Date) ≤ N` is the day of the MONTH, which counts weekends and holidays, and `dayofmonth` has no `EL_FEATURES` row either way; the counter uses only `Month` and `Date`, both registered, and is the more faithful reading of "trading days". `dayIndex` stays 0 until the first month boundary the strategy sees, so the partial month at the chart start never fires. `Date[1]` is data-series history, so no first-bar guard. Not `SeasonalHalfYear` (month), not `DayOfWeekEntry`/`DayOfWeekAnyOf` (weekday) |

## Not written

- `HorizontalFlagWidthTest` — duplicate of `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple` (`rules/RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple.json`, slice 11, on disk), per the orchestrator's decision. That rule is `Highest(High, N) − Lowest(Low, N) ≤ K × ATR(M)` with `rangeLookback`/`rangeAtrMultiple`/`atrLookback`; this key is the identical condition with `flagBars`/`flatAtr`/`atrLookback`. `NarrowRangeWidth` (slice 06) was already held back against the same rule.
- `CloseAboveHighestClose` — duplicate of `Breakout` (`rules/Breakout.json`) at `lookback = N + 1`. `Breakout` computes the highest close over bars `0..lookback-1`, current bar INCLUDED, and fires on `el_eq(close[0], highestClose)`; because the current close is inside its own window that is exactly "close is at or above the highest close of bars `1..lookback-1`". This key is "close is at or above the highest close of bars `1..N`", i.e. the same test with `lookback = N + 1`, tolerance behaviour included — the two fire on identical bars, and both reach back the same number of bars. The merge pass kept it on the strength of the `[1]` exclusion convention, but that convention only distinguishes a rule when the price field differs, which is why `CloseAbovePriorHighestHigh` (highs, not closes) survives the same comparison and this does not.
- `OppositeCloseCountBelowThreshold` — duplicate of `OppositeBodyCountInWindow` (`rules/OppositeBodyCountInWindow.json`, slice 12, on disk), confirmed as the orchestrator asked. That rule is `CountIF(Close < Open, lookback) <= maxOpposite` long and the bull mirror short, over a current-inclusive window, defaults `lookback(10)`/`maxOpposite(3)`. This key is `CountIF(Close < Open, windowBars) < maxOppositeBodies` with defaults `windowBars(10)`/`maxOppositeBodies(3)`. The counted predicate, the window, the mirroring and the defaults are identical; the comparison differs only in strictness, and on an integer count `< K` is `<= K − 1`, so it is the existing rule at a shifted parameter. The merge pass's `catalog_match` pointed at `TailBarCountDominance` instead, which is a different (wick-shaped, bull-vs-bear) count.

## Notes for the orchestrator

- No rule was blocked on an unregistered EasyLanguage word, and none leans on an `ASSUMED` register row. The words used are `average`, `highest`/`lowest`, `countif`, `maxlist`/`minlist`, `absvalue`, `month`, `date`, `ticks`, `volume`, `wfsafe_avgtruerange` and the price series — all VERIFIED or ACCEPTED.
- Two rules were re-expressed to stay off UNKNOWN words, both flagged in their rows: `PriorSessionLevelTest`'s tick tolerance became a fraction of ATR (`MinMove`/`PriceScale` are UNKNOWN and the engine exposes no tick size), and `FirstTradingDayOfMonthFilter` counts trading days from `Month`/`Date` rather than calling `DayOfMonth`, which has no register row.
- Nothing outside the EasyLanguage translation table: no `std::` containers, no lambdas, no ternaries or `auto` in a condition, no `classMembersHook`. `PriorSessionLevelTest`'s ATR block reuses `AtrProfitTarget`'s ternaries inside its hook verbatim, as `CloseWithinAtrOfVwap` already does.
- `AverageRangeBelowDollarThreshold` keeps the merged key even though its threshold carries no `BigPointValue` and so is not in dollars unless the instrument is $1 per point. Renaming it `AverageRangeBelowThreshold` would be more honest; the key was left alone because the orchestrator indexes by it.
- `VolumeBelowAverageFraction` is `VolumeBelowAverage` generalized with a fraction input (and `<=` where that rule has `<`). The merge note suggested amending the existing rule instead, which the brief forbids, so it is a second file. Precedent for carrying both: `Momentum`/`DualMomentum`, `ProfitProtector`/`ProfitProtectorRatio`, `RangeWidthBelowAtrMultiple`/`TightRangeBlocksEntries`. If the orchestrator would rather amend `VolumeBelowAverage`, this is the cheapest rule in the slice to drop.
- `DeepPullbackRetracementFilter` has a third near neighbour that is not yet on disk: `CloseInRangePosition` (slice 02) and `RangeExtremeFade` (slice 11, held back against it) are both "the close sits in the bottom (long) / top (short) fraction of the N-bar high-low range", which is this rule's arithmetic with the two sides EXCHANGED and a current-inclusive window. Slice 13's `PullbackDepthFromRecentHigh` row already vetted this key and kept it, and slice 13's `DistanceAboveRecentLow` row sets the precedent that a side exchange is not the negate toggle. Worth a look when slice 02 lands.
- `PauseBarBroad` takes `dojiBodyFraction(0.2)`, `Barbwire`'s and `DojiBar`'s value, not the merged slice's 0.25 — the merge note asked for whatever `DojiBar` settles on.
- `AdverseBodyCountExit` and `OutsideOutsidePattern` carry no `lag` input, unlike the sibling pattern rules `IoiPattern`/`OioPattern`/`InsideBarInclusive`. That follows the merged spec, which lists no parameters; say the word if the family should be uniform.
