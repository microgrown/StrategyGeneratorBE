# Slice 02 — bar anatomy, reversal / signal bars

CATALOG rows for the twelve rules written from `slices/02_bar.json`.

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SpikePauseBar` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | none | The bar failed to make a higher high OR closed below its own open (short: no lower low, or closed above its open) — Brooks's pause that ends a bull spike. An OR, so it is not assemblable from the ANDing entry pane and is carried whole. Stated positively as "the spike paused"; a with-trend placement negates it. Not `HigherLowBar` or `LaggedHigherHigh` — both halves must fail for this to be false, which no conjunction of those reaches |
| `ThinAreaLowBarOverlap` | Entry | same | `spikeBars` | `spikeBars(3)`, `overlapFraction(0.25)` | EVERY one of the last N bar pairs overlaps the earlier bar by at most fraction F of THAT bar's range: `CountIF(MinList(High, High[1]) − MaxList(Low, Low[1]) <= F × (High[1] − Low[1]), spikeBars)` must equal the window. Brooks's thin one-sided breakout area. Multiplied form, so a zero-range earlier bar cannot divide by zero and no ε is needed; a gap passes the pair, which is the intent. NOT `OverlappingBarRun` negated — negating an all-pairs conjunction gives "at least one pair overlaps little", not "every pair does", so no placement toggle reaches it. Not `AverageBarOverlap` (a mean: one wide pair fails this and barely moves that) and not `BarOverlapFraction` (a single pair) |
| `AverageUpperTailFraction` | Entry | mirrored | `windowBars - 1` | `windowBars(10)`, `maxTailFraction(0.3)`, `epsilon(0.000001)` | The mean UPPER-tail fraction over the last N bars, current bar included, is below T — as a long filter, the absence of persistent selling pressure; the short side reads the mean lower tail. `Average((High − MaxList(Open, Close)) / (High − Low + ε), windowBars)`. A plain `Average` over a price EXPRESSION, so a fresh loop with real history — not `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` and not a zero-filled variable mirror. The ε is `CloseRangePosition`'s literal, carried as an input, and guards a zero-range bar (EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero on a zero denominator). Not `TailBarCountDominance` (a CountIF of tail-vs-body bars, and a contest between the two directions) |
| `BarRangeBelowAverageMultiple` | Entry | same | `avgRangeLookback` | `avgRangeLookback(20)`, `maxRangeMultiple(1.5)` | This bar's high−low range is at most K × `Average(High − Low, avgRangeLookback)[1]`, the mean range over the window ENDING ONE BAR AGO, so an oversized signal bar does not raise its own benchmark. A size cap on the signal bar: too big and the entry sits too far from the level being faded. Not `NarrowRangeBar`, whose window is current-inclusive and whose multiplier is a fraction. Not `BarRangeAboveStd` negated — that band is mean + K standard deviations over a mirrored variable history, and its sd term moves with the volatility of volatility. Not `AtrDollarsBelow` (ATR in dollars, not this bar against the mean range) |
| `BreakoutBarLargeBodyLargeRange` | Entry | same | `rangeLookback` | `strongBodyFraction(0.7)`, `rangeLookback(10)` | The bar's body is at least F of its own range AND its range is at or above `Average(High − Low, rangeLookback)[1]`, the mean over the window ending one bar ago — the book's strong breakout bar. `AbsValue` of the body, so identical both sides. Kept whole: neither half stands alone in the source, and the pair is the single named claim ("a large trend body, and the larger the bar the more likely the breakout succeeds"). Not `ClimaxBar` (K × ATR and a direction-carrying trend-bar test) and not `BarRangeAboveStd` (mean + K sd, no body term) |
| `BullReversalBarAtNewLow` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback` | `lookback(5)`, `closeFraction(0.6)` | The bar's low is at or below `Lowest(Low, lookback)[1]` — the lowest low of the N bars ENDING ONE BAR AGO — and it closes above its open with the close at least F of the way up its own range (short: a new N-bar high, a bear body, close low in the range). A bull reversal bar that actually reversed something. `Highest`/`Lowest` written out as the `Extremes` loop they bottom out in (seeded at the window's first bar, strict scan, EL-source tolerance). Not `CloseRangePosition`, which has no way to express the new-N-bar-low term; not `Breakout` negated (that reads closes, this reads the low against prior lows) |
| `BullReversalBarWithTail` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | `closePositionMin(0.7)`, `lowerTailMin(0.2)` | Bull close, the close at least F of the way up the bar's own range, and a lower tail of at least T of that range (short: bear close, close low in the range, upper tail at least T). Brooks's reversal bar read as close position plus rejection tail. The close-position term alone is `CloseRangePosition(0.7)` without its ε; the tail requirement is what the catalog has no rule for and is what makes it a reversal rather than just a strong close. Near-disjoint from a small-tail strong-trend-bar test, which REQUIRES the tail to be small |
| `CloseInRangePosition` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(20)`, `bottomFraction(0.2)` | The close sits in the bottom fraction F of the N-bar high-low range (short: the top fraction): `Close − Lowest(Low, N) <= F × (Highest(High, N) − Lowest(Low, N))`, windows current-bar-inclusive. Fading a range extreme with no reversal-bar requirement. Written multiplied rather than as the stochastic ratio so an N-bar block of identical bars cannot divide by zero — no ε input; at HH = LL both sides fire. Not `CloseRangePosition` (the CURRENT BAR's own range), not `ClosePercentileRank` (ranks the close among the last N CLOSES by counting — wicks move this and not that), not `RangeMidpointZone` (a band around the midpoint). Absorbs slice 11's `RangeExtremeFade` |
| `ExhaustionBarAfterLargeTrendBar` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `bodyShrink(0.5)`, `tailFraction(0.4)` | After a bear trend bar, this bar's body is at most S × that prior body AND it shows a lower tail of at least T of its own range (short: after a bull bar, an upper tail) — exhaustion, read as a countertrend entry. The prior body is read directly rather than against a window average, which is what the shrink ratio already measures, so the rule needs no window. `MinList`/`MaxList` only to find the body's edge. Distinct from `SmallBodyLargeOppositeTailBar`, which is about a single bar's own shape; this one is relative to the bar before it |
| `GrowingTopTails` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | none | The bar has a strictly positive top tail and that tail is larger than the prior bar's top tail (short: bottom tails). A first DIFFERENCE of tail size, not a level, so no threshold on any other tail rule reaches it — a run of small but growing top tails fires here and fails `AverageUpperTailFraction` and `UpperTailCountInWindow`. The strictly-positive term keeps two consecutive shaved tops from reading as growth. Long side is the bearish reading (tails forming at the top of a move), so a with-trend placement negates it |
| `LargeOppositeTailWeakensReversal` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | `largeTailFraction(0.35)`, `minBodyOverride(0.4)` | The bar does NOT have a large tail against the entry direction (top tail for a long, bottom tail for a short), OR its body is still at least B of its range: `NOT(topTail >= L × range) OR AbsValue(Close − Open) >= B × range`. An OR with an override clause, so not assemblable from panes; stated positively (the bar is acceptable), and a placement wanting Brooks's veto negates it. Distinct from `ReversalBarTailFraction`'s opposite-tail cap, which is unconditional with no body escape hatch |
| `LowHoldsPriorClose` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `tolerancePoints(0.25)` | The bar's low never traded more than X price points below the previous bar's close (short: the high never traded X above it) — the buyers did not let the prior close be tested. The merged `toleranceTicks(1)` became a PRICE-POINT input: `MinMove`/`PriceScale` are UNKNOWN in the register and the engine exposes no tick size, so the default is the book's one tick at the Emini's 0.25 points and is instrument-specific — a grid-pass concern. Not `CloseNearPriorLow` (the close against the prior LOW) and not `CloseAbovePriorHigh` (no offset, opposite sense) |

A_KEYS describes slice 08's key as "the bar is a strong trend bar: a with-trend body that is at least F of the bar's range, and both tails no larger than T of the range" — which is this rule's condition term for term, with the same two degrees of freedom, and on the long side "with-trend body" and this rule's explicit `Close > Open` pick out the same bars. The 0.6 / 0.2 defaults are also the corpus-wide strong-trend-bar definition already cited in `ClimaxBar`'s row. `StrongTrendBarRun` (slice 08) at `consecutiveBars = 1` is a third statement of it. - `AverageUpperTailFraction` vs `AverageTailFractionBelow` (slice 04, not yet on disk): both average a tail fraction over an N-bar window. They are not the same statistic — slice 04's averages the WHOLE non-body part of each bar (both tails together) and is direction-agnostic; this one averages ONE tail and is mirrored, so it reads selling pressure rather than indecision. Worth a look when slice 04 lands, since a "total tail" rule plus a negate toggle nearly covers the "urgency" use of both.
- `BullReversalBarWithTail` vs `ReversalBarTailFraction` (slice 03, not yet on disk): slice 03's key bands the lower tail between a third and a half and caps the upper one; this rule has a lower bound on the lower tail and a close-position term instead of an upper cap. Different conditions, but the same book paragraph — if slice 03's banded form is authored, check that the pair is not being read as one setup twice.
- `BarRangeBelowAverageMultiple` vs `SmallWeakSignalBarBlock` (slice 03, not yet on disk): A_KEYS describes slice 03's key as this test ANDed with a small-body test inside a negation. The bare range test is carried here on purpose; slice 03's is the composite with the body escape hatch, the same shape as `LargeOppositeTailWeakensReversal`.

## Divergences and conventions

- Tick units. `LowHoldsPriorClose`'s merged `toleranceTicks(1)` became `tolerancePoints(0.25)`, per AUTHORING.md — `minmove` and `pricescale` are UNKNOWN in the register and `ctx` exposes no tick size at all. No other rule in the slice reached for ticks.
- Sides where the merged pseudocode states the BEARISH reading on the long side (`SpikePauseBar`, `GrowingTopTails`): written exactly as the slice states them, and the row says the with-trend use is the negated placement. That follows `LargeOutsideBar`'s and `RangeMidpointZone`'s precedent of authoring the positive composite and negating at placement, and it keeps the mirror honest — the short side is the same reading at the bottom of a move, not the same text copied over.
- Division is avoided wherever a multiplication says the same thing (`ThinAreaLowBarOverlap`, `CloseInRangePosition`, every body- and tail-fraction test), because EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero error on a zero denominator. The one unavoidable ratio, `AverageUpperTailFraction`'s per-bar tail fraction, carries `CloseRangePosition`'s `epsilon(0.000001)` as an input.
- `maxlist` / `minlist` are used only to select among raw opens, closes, highs and lows — exactly the tick-grid price scope their ACCEPTED row allows — and the C++ stand-ins are plain comparisons for that reason.
- `Highest` / `Lowest` are written out as the `Extremes` loop they bottom out in, seeded at the window's first bar with a strict, toleranced scan (`el_lt` / `el_gt`), per the register's ACCEPTED row and `Breakout`'s comment.
- `Average` over a price expression is a FRESH loop summed newest-first, never `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` and never a mirrored variable history: nothing in this slice averages an EL variable, so `BarRangeAboveStd`'s zero-filled buffer is not needed anywhere.
- Every scratch value in a hook is a declared local rather than a hook-scope `double`, so a second placement of the same rule gets its own prefixed copy, and every one is reset at the top of the hook.
- No rule in the slice reached for an unregistered EasyLanguage word, and none leans on an ASSUMED row. The words used are `average`, `countif`, `highest`, `lowest`, `maxlist`, `minlist`, `absvalue` and the price series — all VERIFIED or ACCEPTED. Nothing outside the skill's EL translation table was needed; the C++ uses no containers, lambdas, `auto`, ternaries or `classMembersHook`.
