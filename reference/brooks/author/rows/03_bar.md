# Slice 03 — bar anatomy, reversal / signal bars

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `OpenNearHighCloseNearLow` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | `nearExtreme(0.8)`, `epsilon(0.000001)` | The bar opened in the bottom 1 − N of its own range AND closed in the top N of it (short: opened near the high, closed near the low) — a trend day read off one bar. Both positions use `CloseRangePosition`'s ratio and its literal ε, carried as an input, so the low bits match that rule; the ε also stands in for the zero-range guard, since EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero. Not `CloseRangePosition`: that rule places only the CLOSE and has no input for the open's position, and the entry pane cannot supply one |
| `PullbackNotAfterStrongOppositeReversalBar` | Entry | mirrored | `maxScanBars + 1` | `bodyFraction(0.6)`, `tailFraction(0.2)`, `maxScanBars(10)` | Walk back from bar 1 while the bars close down; the oldest bar of that run started the pullback. The filter PASSES unless that bar was a strong bear reversal bar — body at least F of its range, both tails at most T of it — that also made a higher high than the bar before it (short: a run of up closes, a strong bull bar making a lower low). Passes trivially when bar 1 is not a down-close bar. The scan is capped so Max Bars Back is fixed; the shape test is `StrongTrendBar`'s, written out because it is evaluated at an offset the rule has to FIND, which no `lag` input reaches. Stated in the passing sense, so a placement wanting Brooks's veto negates it |
| `ReversalBarFailure` | Entry | mirrored | 1 | `closePositionMin(0.7)`, `lowerTailMin(0.2)` | The prior bar was a bear reversal bar (`BullReversalBarWithTail`'s short shape, at offset 1 and with that rule's inputs and defaults) and THIS bar trades above its high — the failure traps the shorts, so it is the LONG signal; the mirror fades a failed bull reversal bar. The sides are inverted relative to the reversal-bar primitive it reads, which is the whole point. The source's `Close < Low[1]` variant lost to `Low < Low[1]`, matching the book's "trades below". Not `FailedBreakoutReversal`, which fires off a window extreme rather than a single bar's shape |
| `ReversalBarMinimum` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | none | The bar closed above its own open OR above its own midpoint (short: below either) — Brooks's floor test for a signal bar, the most-cited primitive in the bucket and stated with no parameters in any of its four sources. The disjuncts are `ConsecutiveUpBars(1)` and `CloseRangePosition(0.5)`, but the entry pane ANDs, so their OR is not assemblable and the rule is carried whole. The midpoint is `(high + low) / 2`, a constant denominator, so no divide-by-zero risk |
| `ReversalBarReversesPriorBars` | Entry | mirrored | `max(closeLookback, highLookback)` | `closeLookback(8)`, `highLookback(5)` | The close is above `Highest(Close, closeLookback)[1]` AND the high is above `Highest(High, highLookback)[1]` — both windows END ONE BAR AGO, with independent lengths (short: below the two `Lowest` windows, on closes and lows). Brooks's strength gauge for a reversal bar, and his score form is this test at the two lengths set equal. Not `CloseAbovePriorHighestHigh` (one window, close against HIGHS) and not `Breakout` (closes, current-bar-inclusive, and `>=` where this is strict). `Highest`/`Lowest` written out as the `Extremes` loop they bottom out in, seeded at bar 1 with a strict toleranced scan |
| `ReversalBarStrongerThanBreakoutBar` | Entry | mirrored | `breakoutLookback + 1` | `breakoutLookback(10)`, `strengthRatio(1)` | The prior bar was a bear trend bar closing below `Lowest(Low, breakoutLookback)[2]` — the window ending one bar before IT — and this bar is a bull bar whose body is at least K times that breakout bar's body (short is the mirror). A failed breakout worth fading, measured by BODY SIZE alone. Not `StopRunReversalBar`, which asks whether the close got back above the broken level and says nothing about the two bodies; negate for the source's continuation reading. The level is a constant-offset history reference, so the built-in stands in the EL twin |
| `ReversalBarTailFraction` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | `minTailFraction(0.33)`, `maxTailFraction(0.5)`, `oppositeTailMax(0.1)` | The lower tail is BETWEEN minTailFraction and maxTailFraction of the bar's range and the upper tail is at most oppositeTailMax of it (short: the bands on the upper tail, the cap on the lower). Brooks's "a lower tail about one-third to one-half the height of the bar and a small or nonexistent upper tail", stated four times across three volumes. Tails measured from the body edge, `MinList`/`MaxList` over `Open`/`Close`, so the reading is right for either body colour and the rule carries no body-colour term. Not `BullReversalBarWithTail` (a bull close plus a close-position term and only a FLOOR on the lower tail — no upper cap, no upper bound), not `SmallTailsBar` (both tails small), not `LargeOppositeTailWeakensReversal` (an OR with a body escape hatch) |
| `SmallBodyLargeOppositeTailBar` | Entry | mirrored | 0 | `bodyFraction(0.6)`, `tailFraction(0.3)` | The bar closed UP, its body is strictly under F of its range, and it carries a tail of at least T of that range ABOVE the body — against the direction it closed (short: a down close with a large lower tail). Brooks's weak breakout bar. The body cap is strict and the tail floor is not, as the sources state them. Not `BullReversalBarShape` negated: this requires a with-trend close AND a specific opposite tail, where the negation only asks for the absence of strength. | `SmallTailsBar` | Entry | same | 0 | `tailFraction(0.15)` | Both tails are at most F of the bar's range: `High − MaxList(Open, Close)` and `MinList(Open, Close) − Low`, each against `F × (High − Low)`. Not `StrongTrendBar`, which adds a with-trend BODY floor and is therefore directional — a shaved doji passes this and fails that, and no parameter setting of that rule drops its body-colour term. ## Not written

- `ReversalBarLowOverlap` — duplicate of `BarOverlapFraction` negated. That rule computes `ov = MinList(High, High[1]) − MaxList(Low, Low[1])`, clamps a gap to 0, and fires on `ov >= T × (High[1] − Low[1])`; this key is the same overlap against the same divisor with the comparison reversed. Its catalog row already says "place negated for the spike (little-overlap) sense", and the gap case agrees too: the clamp makes the negation true on a gap, and the unclamped `<=` form is true there as well. The merge pass recorded `catalog_match: none`, which is what it missed. The book's V00-33 prior-two-bars form at 0.3 is covered by `BarOverlapWithPriorRange(lookback=1)`.
- `SignalBarBodyMatchesTradeDirection` — suspected duplicate, stopped for a decision. `close[lag] > open[lag]` is `StrongTrendBar(bodyFraction = 0, tailFraction = 1, lag = lag)` exactly: at those settings the body floor reads `close − open >= 0` and both tail caps read `tail <= range`, all three vacuous given the body-colour term, and `StrongTrendBar` already carries a `lag` input added for precisely this reason ("so the several sources that test the PRIOR bar use this rule at lag 1 rather than a second rule"). At lag 0 it is additionally `ConsecutiveUpBars(1)`. The merge pass recorded `relation: parameterization` against `ConsecutiveUpBars` and judged the `lag` input new; it could not see slice 08's `StrongTrendBar`, which supplies the lag. Two ways out, both the orchestrator's call: accept the degenerate corner of `StrongTrendBar` (bodyFraction 0 and tailFraction 1 are outside any sane grid, which is the argument FOR writing this rule), or add a `lag` input to `ConsecutiveUpBars`, which this slice may not edit. Note that slice 07's `07_breakouts.md` row already names `SignalBarBodyMatchesTradeDirection` "at lag 0" as an existing peer, so that row needs a word changing if the rule stays unwritten.

## Flagged for the orchestrator (written, but check against a sibling slice)

- `ReversalBarTailFraction` vs `BullReversalBarWithTail` (slice 02, on disk) — checked against the JSON, not the description, and they are distinct. Slice 02's rule is a conjunction of a bull CLOSE, a close-position floor and a lower-tail floor. This one has no body-colour term, BOUNDS the lower tail above as well as below, and caps the opposite tail — the two book sentences are different sentences, and neither rule is the other at any parameter setting (no setting of `closePositionMin` produces an upper-tail cap, and no setting here produces a close-position test). They will co-fire often on a genuine reversal bar, which is expected of two readings of the same bar.
- `SmallWeakSignalBarBlock` vs `BarRangeBelowAverageMultiple` (slice 02, on disk) — distinct, and slice 02's own note anticipated this. The shared half is the indexed-`Average` range test, written identically here (fresh newest-first loop over `high[1 + i] − low[1 + i]`), but this rule negates that test ANDed with a body test, giving an OR that no negate toggle on slice 02's rule reaches. Defaults differ deliberately: 0.6 × a 10-bar mean here (the source's "much smaller than recent bars"), 1.5 × a 20-bar mean there (a size CAP, not a smallness test).
- `SmallTailsBar` vs `StrongTrendBar` (slice 08, on disk) — written, because `StrongTrendBar`'s long side requires `close > open` and no parameter setting removes that, so the direction-agnostic tails-only test is unreachable from it or from any AND of panes. Worth a second look if slice 08 later drops the body-colour term.
- `PullbackNotAfterStrongOppositeReversalBar` inlines `StrongTrendBar`'s exact shape test at a dynamic offset, and `ReversalBarFailure` inlines `BullReversalBarWithTail`'s at offset 1, both with those rules' input names and defaults. If either sibling's shape definition changes, these two must change with it.
- `BullReversalBarShape` (slice 02, not written) was this slice's `depends_on` for `PullbackNotAfterStrongOppositeReversalBar`. Its shape is carried inline here using `StrongTrendBar`'s definition, which is what slice 02 called it a duplicate of, so nothing is lost.

## Divergences and conventions

- No rule in the slice reached for an unregistered EasyLanguage word and none leans on an `ASSUMED` row. The words used are `average`, `absvalue`, `highest`, `lowest`, `maxlist`, `minlist`, `not` and the price series — all VERIFIED or ACCEPTED.
- `maxlist` / `minlist` appear only over raw `Open`/`Close`, exactly the tick-grid price scope their ACCEPTED row allows; the C++ stand-ins are plain ternaries for that reason, and they live in `preConditionHook` so no condition carries a bare relational operator.
- Division is avoided wherever a multiplication says the same thing, because EL raises a runtime divide-by-zero on a zero denominator. The one exception is `OpenNearHighCloseNearLow`, which keeps `CloseRangePosition`'s ratio and its `epsilon` input on purpose, so the two rules' low bits agree; `ReversalBarMinimum`'s `/ 2` is a constant denominator.
- `Highest` / `Lowest` are written out in the C++ as the `Extremes` loop they bottom out in, seeded at the window's first bar with a strict toleranced scan (`el_gt` / `el_lt`), per the register's ACCEPTED row and `Breakout`'s comment. Where the EL twin needs the value at a CONSTANT offset (`[1]`, `[2]`) the built-in stands, following `CloseAbovePriorHighestHigh` and `FailedFailureResumption`; nothing here indexes a function call at a variable offset.
- `Average` over a price expression is a fresh loop summed newest-first, never `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` and never a mirrored variable history — no rule in this slice averages an EL variable, so `BarRangeAboveStd`'s zero-filled ring buffer is not needed anywhere.
- Every scratch value is a declared local (so a second placement gets its own prefixed copy) and every one is reset at the top of the hook — `PullbackNotAfterStrongOppositeReversalBar` resets all seven, including the two `bool` scan flags whose initial `true` would otherwise persist.
- Ternaries are the only C++ construct here with no EasyLanguage counterpart, and only as the `MinList`/`MaxList` stand-in that `BarOverlapFraction` and `ConsecutiveBarsLittleOverlap` already established.
- No tick-unit parameter appeared in the slice, so no `MinMove` / `PriceScale` substitution was needed.
