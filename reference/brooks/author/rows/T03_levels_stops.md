# CATALOG rows — slice T03_levels_stops (tick-unit levels, micro structure, two stops)

Nine keys in the slice; eight written, both twins each; one blocked. Rows are in
`rules/CATALOG.md` table format, ready to append. Every tolerance and offset here
is a genuine `ticks` input, written division-first in both twins
(`n * (MinMove / PriceScale)` / `n * (ctx.MinMove() / ctx.PriceScale())`) into a
per-bar `tickSize` local, per the tick-unit addendum; `minmove` and `pricescale`
are VERIFIED. The slice's touch convention is one-sided: a level rule tests
whether the bar REACHED the level with a tick margin, never a two-sided band on
the close.

| Rule | Type | Sides | Reaches back | Inputs | Fires when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `TouchOfRecentExtreme` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback` | `lookback(20)`, `toleranceTicks(1)` | The bar's high trades up to within `toleranceTicks` of the highest high of the `lookback` bars ENDING ONE BAR AGO (short: the low down to the lowest low) — the recent swing extreme as a magnet, and the level a double top or bottom forms at. The window EXCLUDES bar 0, which is what separates it from `HighestHighBreakout`, whose current-inclusive window means "this bar made the N-bar high" and which is this rule's boundary case at `toleranceTicks` 0. Not `NearAnyLevel`, an ATR-toleranced OR across three magnets on the CLOSE. The tolerance band is the whole point: Brooks counts the bars that reach the level, including those stopping a tick short of taking it out, so the test is one-sided with a tick margin (the touch convention this slice follows, from V16-02). The one-bar-shifted PRICE SERIES is handed to `Highest`/`Lowest`, as `StopBeyondRecentRangeExtreme` and `NearAnyLevel` write it, never a lagged read of the function's own result; the C++ mirrors `Extremes()` (seed at the window's first bar, strict, toleranced `el_gt`/`el_lt`, EL source). R09-03's "highest of all failed reversal signal bars across the leg" is a citation only — it needs a persisted set of levels and the window reaches the same price |
| `FibonacciRetracementLevel` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback` | `lookback(20)`, `fibRatio(0.618)`, `toleranceTicks(1)` | The bar's low trades down to within `toleranceTicks` of `windowHigh − fibRatio × (windowHigh − windowLow)` over the `lookback` bars ending ONE BAR AGO (short: the high up to `windowLow + fibRatio × span`). WINDOW ANCHORING, not swing pivots: two of three sources anchor on pivots, one on highest/lowest, and the window wins on mechanizability — it needs no pivot retention and no `pivotStrength`, itself an unmade decision at this level. One rule with the ratio as the parameter (0.618 / 0.5 / 0.62 across the sources; 0.618 is the default, where two of three land); Fibonacci EXTENSIONS (127%, 161.8%) are the same arithmetic at `fibRatio > 1`, so there is no second rule. Not `TwoBarRangePosition` / `CloseRangePosition`, which place the close inside a range as a ratio — this constructs a PRICE from the ratio and asks whether the bar reached it — and not `PullbackDepthFromRecentHigh`, which measures depth without a tick-toleranced touch. Brooks's own scepticism ("most are just approximations and most fail") is a caveat about resting an order there, not about the bar test |
| `ReversalShortOfPriorExtreme` | Entry | mirrored | `recentLookback + levelLookback` | `recentLookback(5)`, `levelLookback(20)`, `untouchedTicks(2)` | Price turned without ever reaching the level: the lowest low of the last `recentLookback` bars stayed at least `untouchedTicks` ABOVE the lowest low of the `levelLookback` bars ending just before that window, and the bar closed up (short: the recent highest high stayed that far BELOW the prior window's high and the bar closed down). THE OPPOSITE POLARITY to the rest of the slice — the signal is that the level was NOT reached — mechanized off the same offset window extreme as `TouchOfRecentExtreme` so the two are directly comparable. SIDES: the merged pseudocode states the SHORT (failing to reach resistance is a sell setup); it is written into the short slot and the long slot carries its mirror, per the corpus convention that `longCondition` is the bullish read. The margin is the pair "strictly short of the level AND at least N ticks short of it", so `untouchedTicks = 0` still means never touched rather than touched-exactly (the `MarginalThreePushBreakout` / `ExpandingTrianglePattern` spelling). The level window is offset by `recentLookback + 1` so it cannot overlap the window tested against it; the offset is applied to the price series inside `Highest`/`Lowest`, not to the function's result. Near neighbour `NearMissLevelReversal` (slice T01) is a two-sided BAND around the prior-window low on THIS bar's low plus a bull body — a single-bar near-miss where this is a multi-bar failure to approach — kept separate as the merge pass directed, and worth the roll-up's comparison |
| `FailedBreakoutBeyondExtreme` | Entry | mirrored | `reverseBars + lookback - 1` | `lookback(10)`, `reverseBars(1)`, `toleranceTicks(0)` | Within the last `reverseBars` bars the low traded below `Lowest(Low[reverseBars], lookback)` and this bar CLOSES back above that level by at least `toleranceTicks` (short: the mirror above the highest high) — Brooks's "the first failed breakout below a prior swing low is a lower low buy setup". The level is an N-bar window extreme, not a pivot: four of six sources state it as a window and it needs no pivot retention, which drops the grade from hard to simple; VP1-13's pivot-anchored variant is reachable by composing `SwingPointUndercut` with a reclaim. Not `FailedBreakoutReversal`, which needs a CLOSING price below the level, re-measures the window per candidate bar, and carries no reclaim margin — this fires on the intrabar low poking through a level fixed once, the far more common bar and the one Brooks describes. Not `StopRunReversalBar` (the breakout bar is specifically the PRIOR bar and a bull body is required) and not `FailedSwingBreakoutReversal` (a confirmed pivot). `reverseBars` 1 is the single-bar rejection all three window sources give; the loop is what makes VP1-13's 3 reachable from the grid. The level window is offset by `reverseBars` so no bar allowed to break it can help define it |
| `MicroDoubleExtreme` | Entry | mirrored | `gap` | `gap(1)`, `toleranceTicks(1)` | This bar's low is within `toleranceTicks` of the low of the bar `gap` bars earlier (short: the highs) — Brooks's "consecutive or nearly consecutive bars with identical or nearly identical lows", and "most small bull reversals come from some type of micro double bottom". One rule with `gap` as the parameter: two sources give the adjacent-bar form (1, the default) and two the one-bar-gap form (2). The tolerance stays in TICKS rather than ATR — all four sources say ticks and Brooks means literally identical or one-tick-apart extremes. T10-08's V-SHAPE conjunct (`Low[1]` above both matching lows, so the pair is a real dip rather than two bars of a slide) is NOT carried: it only bites at `gap ≥ 2` and is a different rule, not a parameter of this one; its close-up conjunct is bar anatomy and a separate placement. Not `Ledge` (a match at any lag inside a window) and not the `DoubleExtreme*` family, which pairs CONFIRMED swing pivots at a variable distance with an ATR tolerance. The measured pivot sense ("a flat run of equal extremes is ONE pivot") does not reach this rule, which uses no pivots |
| `Ledge` | Entry | mirrored | `lookback - 1` | `lookback(5)`, `minMatches(2)`, `toleranceTicks(0)` | At least `minMatches` of the last `lookback` bars have a low within `toleranceTicks` of THE CURRENT BAR's low — a small trading range with a flat floor (short: a flat ceiling of matching highs). ANCHORED, NOT PAIRWISE: two of Brooks's four statements define the ledge pairwise (any two bars in the window matching each other), which is O(n²) and can fire on a level price is nowhere near now; the anchored reading wins on mechanizability and only fires while price is AT the ledge, which is what an entry needs. The count INCLUDES the current bar, which trivially matches itself, exactly as `CountIF` counts bars 0..N−1 inclusive — so `minMatches = 2` means one OTHER bar shares the level and `minMatches = 1` is vacuously true. **Written as an explicit loop in BOTH twins rather than `CountIF`, and that is a specification point, not a style choice**: `CountIF`'s condition is a true SERIES whose offsets shift with the counted bar (VERIFIED), so the anchor — whether written `Low` or parked in a variable — shifts with it and the test degenerates to the distance from a bar to itself, zero on every bar. An anchored count is not expressible with `CountIF`. Tolerance 0 is Brooks's literal "identical lows"; one source says a tick. R28-23's limit a tick or two above the ledge bottom is not representable under the signal-bar-close / next-open convention and is recorded, not carried. Not `MicroDoubleExtreme`, which needs the match at exactly `gap`; the two coincide at `lookback` 2 / `minMatches` 2 |
| `StopBeyondSignalBar` | Exit | mirrored | 1 | `stopOffsetTicks(1)` | The initial protective stop sits `stopOffsetTicks` beyond the SIGNAL bar's extreme opposite the trade — long exits when the close falls to or below `signalLow − stopOffsetTicks × tick` — and stays there for the life of the trade unless a tightening rule, a separate placement, replaces it. Five sources, the single most-cited Brooks stop, all at one tick. THE SIGNAL BAR IS CAPTURED ONCE ON THE FILL BAR (`BarsSinceEntry = 0`, VERIFIED) as `low[1]`/`high[1]` rather than re-read every bar at `low[BarsSinceEntry + 1]`: the same price, but it holds the deepest subscript at 1 instead of the length of the longest trade, which Max Bars Back would otherwise have to cover — the construction `StopAtSignalBarRangeMultiple` and `StopFractionOfSignalBarRisk` already use. While flat it re-captures every bar, which is harmless and is what leaves the value right on the fill bar; `BarsSinceEntry` restarts through a reversal, so nothing uses a `MarketPosition = 0` reset. Not `StopLoss` (fixed dollars — this one's dollar risk varies trade by trade), not `StopFractionOfSignalBarRisk` (a fraction of the way BACK toward the entry, whose `stopFraction = 0` corner is this rule at `stopOffsetTicks = 0`), not `StopAtSignalBarRangeMultiple` (the signal bar's RANGE converted to dollars). T21-73's "keep it there until the second leg begins" is guidance about not trailing, not a condition. Close-based; intrabar stop fills are not modelled |

- `RoundNumberProximity` — **blocked on a rounding word**. The rule needs the
  distance from the close to the nearest multiple of `roundStep`, which requires
  `Round`, `IntPortion`, `Floor` or `Mod`. `rules/EL_FEATURES.md` has NO row for
  `round`, `intportion`, `floor`, `ceiling` or `fracportion`, and `mod` is
  **UNKNOWN** (sign behaviour on negative operands unmeasured), so there is no
  register-clean spelling. Per the tick-unit addendum no probe was written. This
  is the same blocker `NearAnyLevel` records for its unwritten fourth OR term,
  and when the word is measured both can be closed together — `NearAnyLevel`
  gains a `roundStep` term and this rule becomes writable as stated
  (`AbsValue(Close − Round(Close / roundStep) × roundStep) ≤ toleranceTicks × tick`).
  Note that `roundStep` is instrument-specific either way (100 suits a
  three-figure stock, 1000 the Dow, a futures contract something else), and the
  merge pass already dropped V22-03's 5–10% overshoot target as a per-instrument
  judgement rather than a computation.
