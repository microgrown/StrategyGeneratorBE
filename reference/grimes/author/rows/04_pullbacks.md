| `LevelBreakAndRecover` | Entry | mirrored | `pullbackLookback + 1` | `pullbackLookback(10)` | Grimes's first pullback trigger (G06-03): the line bounding the pullback is penetrated and price recovers straight back across it. Mechanized as the PREVIOUS bar trading below the lowest low of the `pullbackLookback` bars ending TWO bars ago, while THIS bar closes back above that same level; the short mirrors on the highs. The bounding line is the SHIFTED WINDOW EXTREME (merge decisions 7 and 20 — the window extreme, never a drawn or projected line); `MicroTrendLineBreak` is the projected-line alternative and is a different rule. The window is shifted by TWO so the breaking bar's own low cannot define the level it is said to have broken. Not `StopRunReclaimReentry`, whose level is a CONFIRMED SWING PIVOT and whose trigger is this bar taking out the prior bar's high by a tick — a different level read on a different bar. Not `DipBelowPriorLowCloseBackAbove`, whose level is the single prior bar's low and whose dip and recovery are the SAME bar. KEPT WHOLE like a crossing: no pane-AND reaches it, because every entry pane evaluates on the same bar and the break is tested on bar 1 while the recovery is tested on bar 0. `Highest`/`Lowest` written out as `Extremes`' seeded strict-comparison scan, `el_gt`/`el_lt` because `Extremes` is EL source |
| `EqualHighsBreakout` | Entry | mirrored | `equalBarsCount` | `equalBarsCount(2)`, `toleranceTicks(2)` | The `equalBarsCount` bars ENDING ONE BAR AGO all share the same high to within `toleranceTicks`, and this bar CLOSES above the highest of them — Grimes's precise entry timing inside a pullback (G06-23); the short mirrors on equal lows with a close below them. KEPT WHOLE like a crossing: the flat level and the close through it are one event, and no pane-AND reaches it because `MicroDoubleExtreme` states the matching-extremes half alone AND assigns the sides the other way round (matching LOWS on the long side, the micro double bottom), so an AND would pair the wrong level with the wrong trigger; `Ledge` is the anchored multi-bar form of the same flat level and is likewise not the trigger. THE TOLERANCE STAYS IN TICKS, as `MicroDoubleExtreme`'s does — Grimes means literally equal highs, and a tick count passes the shift test because a tick is a constant of the symbol. Tick written division-first, `MinMove / PriceScale`, read once per bar. The window ends one bar ago so the breakout bar's own high cannot be one of the highs it is breaking, and the equality is measured against `High[1]`, the NEWEST bar of the run, so the run is a band of width `toleranceTicks` around that bar rather than a drifting chain of pairwise comparisons. The loop runs without an early exit and clears a flag, matching the EL twin statement for statement |
| `GapHoldsUnfilled` | Entry | mirrored | `fillLookback + gapBarOffset` | `fillLookback(5)`, `gapBarOffset(1)` | The bar `fillLookback` bars ago gapped in the trade's direction — its whole range clear of the bar `gapBarOffset` bars before it, `TrueGapBar`'s own non-overlap relation — and no bar since has traded back into it: the lowest low of the `fillLookback` bars since is still above the pre-gap bar's high (G03-34, "are there gaps up that do not fill"); the short mirrors on a gap down. MECHANIZED AT A FIXED LAG rather than by scanning backwards for the most recent gap, which keeps it a plain window rule; the scanned form is `GapFilledExit`'s construction and is the recorded alternative. `fillLookback` therefore does double duty BY CONSTRUCTION — it is both how many bars ago the gap printed and the span of the not-filled test, and those are the same set of bars, since `Lowest(Low, fillLookback)` covers offsets 0..`fillLookback`−1, exactly the bars after the gap bar. `gapBarOffset` is the SPAN OF THE NON-OVERLAP TEST: at the default 1 it is `TrueGapBar`'s relation, and larger values ask the gap bar to stand clear of an earlier bar instead (the merged slice named the input but its pseudocode never used it; this is the reading that makes it non-redundant). Not `GapHoldsAbovePriorSessionClose`, whose reference is the PRIOR SESSION's close and which needs the session machinery — this reference is a BAR gap, so it works at any bar scale and is what Grimes asks on a daily chart. Not `PostGapFadeBlockWindow`, which asks only whether any gap printed in a window and carries no fill test. Both terms are written out because at `gapBarOffset > 1` the gap bar's own low is not inside the `Lowest` window |
| `PullbackRangeBelowLegRange` | Entry | same | `pullbackBars + legBars - 1` | `pullbackBars(5)`, `legBars(10)`, `reducedFraction(0.75)` | The mean high−low range over the `pullbackBars` bars ending at this bar is below `reducedFraction` × the mean high−low range over the `legBars` bars IMMEDIATELY BEFORE THEM — "good pullbacks almost always show reduced activity" (G03-22, G06-15). Not `TrueRangeAvgRatio`, which nests a fast `TrueRange` mean INSIDE a slow one (`lookback` against `lookback × slowFactor`, SAME END BAR), so the fast window's bars are counted twice and the setup leg cannot be isolated; not `BarRangeBelowAverageMultiple`, which sizes ONE bar against a window mean. THE MEANS ARE EXPLICIT LOOPS IN BOTH TWINS rather than `Average(High − Low, n)` and `Average(High − Low, n)[pullbackBars]`: `Average` is VERIFIED as recomputing from scratch every bar over a series with real history, which a loop reproduces exactly, but reading a function call's OWN HISTORY at an offset is unmeasured and the second window needs exactly that. Both loops sum newest bar first in both twins, so the two accumulate identical rounding. Both windows are high−low RANGES, not `TrueRange`, per the merged pseudocode. Sides are identical text — the test carries no direction. Fractions 0.7 (ch3) and 0.75 (ch6); 0.75 default |
| `NoClimaxBarInWindow` | Entry | same | `max(climaxLookback − 1, atrLength + 1)` | `climaxLookback(10)`, `climaxMultiple(2)`, `atrLength(20)`, `bodyFraction(0.6)` | No climax bar of EITHER direction printed in the last `climaxLookback` bars, the climax test being `ClimaxBar`'s term for term (a trend bar whose body is at least `bodyFraction` of its own range and whose range is at least `climaxMultiple` ATRs). Grimes states it as a BLOCK — "do not enter pullbacks following potential climaxes" (G03-09), and ch6 blocks the simple Pullback template while allowing the complex one (G06-29) — but the corpus states every "do not trade while X" claim POSITIVELY and lets the per-placement negate toggle give the block; `PostGapFadeBlockWindow` is the construction copied here, including the positive statement. THE WINDOW FORM CANNOT BE BUILT BY NEGATING `ClimaxBar`: a single placement of that rule fires on ONE bar, so its negation says only that THIS bar is not a climax. Not `ConsecutiveClimaxBarCount`, the count form with a separation rule. Both directions count, so the sides are identical text. ONE ATR value for the whole window, read at the current bar (`LargeBarCountInWindow`'s and `ConsecutiveClimaxBarCount`'s arrangement): the counted condition is an explicit loop rather than `CountIF` precisely so the stateful `WFSafe_AvgTrueRange` accumulator is not re-evaluated at each counted offset, reading its own history at an offset being unmeasured. Loop runs without a break, clearing a flag |
| `HighAndTightFlagSmallBarsNearExtreme` | Entry | mirrored | `max(swingLookback, atrLookback + 1, pullbackBars − 1)` | `pullbackBars(2)`, `smallBarAtrFraction(0.5)`, `nearAtrFraction(1.0)`, `swingLookback(20)`, `atrLookback(14)` | EVERY one of the last `pullbackBars` bars has a range under `smallBarAtrFraction` ATRs AND holds within `nearAtrFraction` ATRs of the highest high of the `swingLookback` bars ending one bar ago — the one- or two-bar "high and tight flag" that is a complete pullback (G01-14); the short mirrors below the recent lowest low. | `AtrAboveAverageMultiple` | Entry | same | `max(atrLookback + 1, lookback)` | `atrLookback(15)`, `lookback(20)`, `atrMultiple(1.5)` | The ATR exceeds `atrMultiple` × its own N-bar average — Grimes's "extremely high volatility going into a pullback" (G05-22), his climax/exhaustion warning that the pullback is about to fail; his reading is about 1.5 (the default) and the grid should run 1.0–2.5. Authored 2026-09-22 as an input on `AtrAboveAverage`, split out 2026-09-27; at `atrMultiple 1.0` it IS `AtrAboveAverage` (`1.0 × x` is exactly `x`). The number of COMPARISONS is `legs − 1`, and the grid runs 2–4. Authored 2026-09-22 as an input on `HigherSwingLow` and split out 2026-11-07, because the 202611 BRK and OWN templates place that rule. THE RUN IS TESTED AS THE SCAN GOES, one comparison per newly found pivot, so `legs` needs no array and is unbounded above. GUARD: "enough pivots" is the COUNT reaching `legs`, never a test of a PRICE against `-1`. The ascending triangle's flat ceiling is `LevelTouchCount` or `Ledge` in its own pane, or `AscendingTriangleIntoLevel` whole. Not `LaggedHigherHigh` or `HigherLowCount`, which compare ADJACENT BARS at a fixed lag |
