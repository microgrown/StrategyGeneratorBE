| `StopAtLowNBarsAgo` | Exit | mirrored | `nBarsAgo` | `nBarsAgo(3)` | A long is closed when the close reaches the low of the bar EXACTLY `nBarsAgo` bars back; the short mirrors on that bar's high. Grimes G08-14, "stop out at the lowest low of three days ago". NOT A TRUE TRAIL, and that is the whole point: because the anchor is one bar and not `Lowest(Low, N)`, the level is not monotone — as the window rolls, a lower bar arriving at offset `nBarsAgo` LOOSENS the stop again. That is the difference from `StopBeyondRecentRangeExtreme`, whose level is the window extreme and moves only with the window, and it is why the single-bar reading of the ambiguous source was kept rather than folded into that rule. Degenerate overlap, recorded not resolved: at `nBarsAgo = 1` this is `TrailArmedAfterProfitThreshold` at `armProfitDollars 0` / `stopOffsetTicks 0` (`MaxPositionProfit` starts at 0, so that arming is always true), so the grid should start at 2. `nBarsAgo` is a `double` input and the subscript narrows, as README "Input variables" allows |
| `StopFractionOfTestedLevelRisk` | Exit | mirrored | `levelLookback` | `stopFraction(0.5)`, `levelLookback(20)` | Grimes's "Stop A", INSIDE the tested level (G04-09): a long exits when the close falls to `EntryPrice − stopFraction × (EntryPrice − levelLow)`, where `levelLow` is the one-bar-shifted `levelLookback`-bar low captured ON THE FILL BAR; the short mirrors above `EntryPrice`. "It virtually eliminates slippage even in thin markets because you are exiting long before the mass of other stop orders is hit." Not `StopFractionOfSignalBarRisk`: identical arithmetic, a different FAR ANCHOR and a different DIRECTION of measurement — that rule measures the fraction UP from the SIGNAL BAR'S low, this one DOWN from the ENTRY PRICE toward the tested RANGE extreme; complementary, both kept. The level is the shifted window extreme (merge decision 7), captured once on `BarsSinceEntry = 0` (VERIFIED as 0 on the fill bar and as restarting through a reversal, which a `MarketPosition = 0` reset would miss), which is what holds "reaches back" at `levelLookback` rather than at the length of the longest trade. No arm flag is needed: `BarsSinceEntry` is 0 on every flat bar too, so the capture is refreshed right up to the fill and never read stale. The `MarketPosition` guard is load-bearing — `EntryPrice` reads 0.0 while flat. The window mirrors `Extremes()`: seeded on the window's first bar, strict comparison so a tie keeps the earliest bar, toleranced `el_lt`/`el_gt` (EL source), as `StopBeyondRecentRangeExtreme` writes it. SHIFT TEST: the level is an affine combination of two prices, so it translates with the series exactly as `Close` does |
| `ProfitTargetAtRiskMultiple` | Exit | mirrored | 0 | `riskMultiple(1)`, `stopDistance(2)` | A long is closed when the BAR'S OWN HIGH reaches `EntryPrice + riskMultiple × stopDistance`; the short mirrors on the bar's low. PARTIAL PROFITS ARE NOT EXPRESSIBLE: each risk multiple is a FULL exit placement (decision 24), and Grimes's 25–50 percent / "more than half" fractions are recorded, not mechanized. Not `TakeProfit`, which fires on `OpenPositionProfit` — the close-marked dollar mark, net of the entry side's costs — so it can only trigger at a bar close and only after costs; this fires on the bar's own EXTREME, the limit fill Grimes describes, and carries no stop leg (`StopLossTakeProfitDollar` ORs one in off the same risk unit).
